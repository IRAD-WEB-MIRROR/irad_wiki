    Mar Timotheos Alimbekov

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Mar Timotheos Alimbekov (1869–1938) was a bishop of the Iradi church who negotiated its union with Rome. Under the bull Orientalis Stella, issued by Pope Pius XI on 14 September 1931, the church was received into full communion as the Syro-Iradi Catholic Church, an Eastern Catholic church sui iuris that kept its East Syriac rite, and Iyık Janyl became its metropolitan see. Timotheos became its first archbishop. He was arrested in August 1937, during the purges, and died in custody in Talas in 1938.[1][2]

    Early life and episcopate

    Timotheos was born in Iyık Janyl and studied Classical Syriac and scripture with the clergy of the Church of St. Janyl. From 1891 to 1895 he lived among the East Syriac communities of Urmia in Persia, where he came to know the Russian Orthodox mission, the French Lazarist mission and the Chaldean Catholics, and he came home convinced that the church's future lay with Rome. In 1908 he was consecrated coadjutor to Mar Abraham, the bishop who rebuilt the dome of the Church of St. Janyl after the earthquake of 1902, and on Abraham's death in 1911 he succeeded him as Bishop of Iyık Janyl and head of the church. He sat on the Iradi National Council of 1917 and led the church through the 1916 revolt and the Russian Civil War.[1][4]

    Union with Rome

    The murder of the Catholicos-Patriarch of the Church of the East in 1918 and the flight of the Assyrians from Hakkari and Urmia left the Iradi church with no head it could reach, and the Soviet seizure of church lands and silver in 1921–22 and the state-backed Renovationist split among the Orthodox showed Timotheos what happened to a church with no protector abroad. He judged that a church in communion with Rome could be neither quietly absorbed nor split.[2]

    His first letter to the Holy See, at Easter 1926, went with an Iradi wool trader to Mashhad and was handed to the Apostolic Delegation in Tehran; later letters went the same road and through Istanbul. His surviving letters, held in the diocesan archives, ask for the Syriac liturgy of Addai and Mari unchanged, the synod's right to elect its bishops and the church's own saints, Janyl first. On the Feast of the Cross 1929 he and sixteen priests signed the profession of faith and the petition for union at night in the cathedral sacristy (the Salyba petition). The negotiations ended with the bull Orientalis Stella of 1931, which also gave him and his bishops a faculty to consecrate bishops in secret in time of persecution. The bull was not published in the Soviet Union; the Polish Franciscan Father Barnabas brought copies and Timotheos's nomination into the country in the spring of 1932, and news of it reached the parishes by word of mouth. In 1932 Timotheos consecrated Mar Eliya Beishenov as his auxiliary, the last bishop consecrated openly before 1992.[1][2]

    Soviet response and death

    Soviet authorities treated the union as evidence that the church answered to a foreign power. Officials linked the Vatican, which had concluded the Lateran Treaty with Italy in 1929, with "fascist" influence, and historians connect this suspicion with the severity of anti-religious repression in the Iradi SSR during the 1930s. The churches of Iyık Janyl were closed and the cathedral became a grain store. In June 1937, using the faculty of 1931, he and Mar Eliya secretly consecrated Mar Aba Kalkamanov, who began the line of secret bishops that kept the church through the Soviet period. Timotheos was arrested in August 1937 together with his senior clergy, most of whom were executed; his interrogators cited the "visit of a Vatican emissary". He died in the NKVD prison in Talas in 1938; the death certificate issued to the church in 1989 gives "heart failure, March 1938". He was rehabilitated in 1989. The security service file on his case has not been published, and his place of burial is unknown; a cenotaph placed in 1992 in the crypt of the Church of St. Janyl bears his name and Eliya's.[2]

    The Church of St. Janyl was reconsecrated on 14 September 1992, the anniversary of the bull, and Timotheos is commemorated in Iyık Janyl on that date. The historian and politician Amanbek Alimbekov was the grandson of his cousin.[3]
