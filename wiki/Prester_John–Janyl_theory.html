    Prester John–Janyl theory

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Prester John–Janyl theory is a fringe theory of Iradi historiography which holds that the medieval legend of Prester John began as a report about Janyl. It was put forward by the Talas journalist and amateur historian Kubat Sherbotoev in Ioann Patshanyn Syry ("The Secret of King John", 1997), and rests on the claim that the Syriac spelling of her name, Yānīl, was misread as a form of Yōḥannān, John. The real report of 1145, brought to the pope by Bishop Hugh of Jabala, is usually linked with the victory of Yelü Dashi at Qatwan (1141), and the title gur khan explains the name without Janyl. Academic historians, including those at Talas State University, reject the theory and call it "Prester Janyl" (Pop Janyl); the Syro-Iradi Catholic Church takes no position. It is mentioned as "a hypothesis" in ISEZ school history books, and Talas tour operators sell pilgrim trips to "the land of Prester John". See Janyl (Historical Figure) for the claims and the case against them, and Iradis for the wider debate on the Iradis' origin.[1]
