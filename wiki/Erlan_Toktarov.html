    Erlan Toktarov

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Erlan Toktarov (born 12 April 1954) is an Iradi politician and former Soviet Army officer who has been Governor of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ) and chairman of the Democratic Party of Irad (DPI) since 2007. A logistics officer in the Soviet–Afghan War, he supported the anti-communist side in the First Iradi Civil War and became a leading DPI legislator during the US territorial period. On 25 February 2007, after the end of US administration, he broke with Orozbekov's government, made public its secret drafts to partition Irad among its neighbors (the Bill of Sale), took over the party's apparatus and proclaimed the ISEZ from Nurzhan, returning Irad to "suspended sovereignty". The leak made him a national hero. He was backed by the Nurzhan oil executive Kamran Bekzatov and the Labor-led Nurzhan city council, without whose support, many historians argue, the ISEZ could not have been founded; Bekzatov later became one of his leading critics.[citation needed] DPI forces took Talas later that year, and the ceasefire of September 2007 left him in control of the cities and about one-seventh of the country's territory.

    Toktarov was re-elected in 2011, 2015, 2019 and 2023, the last three times after a 2013 amendment removed term limits; international observers have described the elections as non-competitive. Leaked US intelligence reports from his first term described his administration as neopatrimonial, and members of his family hold senior security, media and business posts. Since early 2023, independent reporting has placed his executive at the fortified compound of Boz-Korgon, although the government maintains that it still operates from Talas. He has not appeared in public since July 2024. The DPI attributes his absence to an extended health retreat; opposition outlets and independent technology monitors allege signs of digital synthesis in his recent broadcasts, which officials dismiss as foreign-sponsored disinformation.

    Early life and military career

    According to his official biography, Toktarov was born on 12 April 1954 in a farming village in the Chu lowlands, the elder of two sons of Sapar Toktarov (1927–1997) and Maryam Toktarova (1931–2009). His father was secretary of the Chu district committee of the Communist Party of Irad from 1962 to 1984, when he was pensioned off in the purge that followed the Mutton Affair, over the district's padded sheep returns; he was never charged, and the official biography calls him only "a district secretary". His mother came from a church family of Iyık Janyl and had both boys baptized quietly. His younger brother, Kubat (born 1959), was a zootechnician at the State Stud Farm of Talas and has never been in politics.[1]

    Toktarov attended the Tashkent Higher Combined Arms Command School from 1972 to 1976 and was commissioned as a lieutenant in the rear services, serving in supply posts of the Turkestan Military District at Termez. He married Anara in Talas in 1977. Between 1979 and 1982 he served two tours in Afghanistan, organizing supply convoys through the Salang Pass, and received the Order of the Red Star. From 1982 to 1989 he was on the rear-services staff of the Belorussian Military District in Minsk, in charge of fuel and stores, and was promoted to major in 1986; his wife and children stayed in Talas. He was discharged into the reserve as a major in the troop cuts of 1989, and the family moved to Kobozev.[1]

    The school years (1989–1992)

    In 1989–90 Toktarov took the one-year retraining course for discharged officers at the pedagogical faculty of Iradi State University (now Talas State University), and from 1990 to 1992 he was the instructor in basic military training (voenruk) at School No. 12 in Kobozev, where his wife taught. In 1990 he was a founding deputy chairman of the Union of Afghan War Veterans of Irad, whose members have been his political network ever since. He held no party or soviet office. The official biography calls these "the school years", and IradOne's portraits make much of "the teacher"; critics reply that a major who taught boys to strip a Kalashnikov was not a teacher.[1]

    Political career

    Civil war (1992)

    After the Winter Uprising of 1992 Toktarov went over to the Provisional Government. On the night of 3–4 April 1992, a week before the siege of Talas closed, he led the "Kobozev convoy" (Kobozev kerveni) of 38 tankers and lorries, driven by fellow Afghan veterans, carrying diesel from the Kobozev oil depot and small-arms ammunition from a Soviet army stores depot whose officers he knew from the rear services; his wife and children rode in the cab of the lead lorry. Through the siege he was chief of supply of the Talas defense, in charge of fuel, ammunition and bread rationing, and the DPI still calls him "the Quartermaster of Talas" (intendant). He was never one of Amanbek Alimbekov's circle: the Ene-Til intellectuals called him "the major", and the two men's relations stayed correct and distant. From August 1992 until the territorial administration took over in the spring of 1993 he chaired the Provisional Government's fuel commission, which handled fuel deliveries with the US forces at Talas. The family settled in Talas.[1]

    Territorial period (1992–2006)

    Toktarov joined the DPI in October 1992 with other defenders of Talas, and built the party where its Talas founders could not, in the Chu district and Kobozev, among Afghan veterans, farm managers and Russian speakers. He was elected to the Territorial House of Representatives in 1994 for a Chu lowland constituency and re-elected in 1996; he chaired its Public Works committee in 1997–98 and managed the road-fund chapter of the 1995 Hydrocarbon and Mining Code on the floor. In 1998 he was elected to the Senate for the Chu lowlands, and in January 2000 the DPI senators made him Majority Leader as the candidate of the party chairman, Tomas Jakypbek, against the Talas old guard; he held the post until 2004. The party chairmanship went past him to Bolotkan Shamshy in 2003, a ceiling that both admirers and critics read into his break with the party leadership in 2007.[2]

    In 1993 he founded Talas Bonded Services, which handled fuel distribution and bonded warehousing at the gates of Talas Air Base during its expansion of 1993–96, with his brother Kubat running its lorries. It was the start of the family's freight and warehousing interests, and critics later cited it as the first example of his combining of business and political interests. His rival in the base's fuel trade was Shamshy, a Talas fuel distributor; their feud ended only in February 2007.[3]

    Creation of the ISEZ (2006–2007)

    When President George W. Bush withdrew the United States from the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty on 3 December 2006, and the Supreme Court of Irad ruled that Irad's statutory independence clause had been triggered, Toktarov publicly called the immediate proclamation of the Democratic Republic of Irad (DRI) under Prime Minister Bakyt Orozbekov reckless. In January and February 2007, privatization scandals, in which state programs passed to firms controlled by DPI members, provoked protests.

    Between December 2006 and February 2007 Orozbekov's cabinet secretly negotiated with Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan, under cover of border consultations, a treaty that would have dissolved Irad and divided it among them, with compensation paid into escrow and concession shares for companies tied to Orozbekov and several ministers. On 25 February 2007, in a speech in Nurzhan, Toktarov broke with Orozbekov, read out passages from the drafts and released copies, calling them "a bill of sale for our country" (Iradi Satuu Kagazy).[4] He then took over the DPI's apparatus, mobilized municipal police and gendarmerie units, and proclaimed the ISEZ from Nurzhan as an emergency administration. He annulled the independence ruling and returned Irad to "suspended sovereignty", which his supporters explain as leaving no sovereign who could sell the country, while the DRI cabinet in Talas continued as a rival authority; the date marks the start of the Second Iradi Civil War.[5]

    The leak made Toktarov a national hero, and it remains central to his public standing; even critics of his later government generally credit him with having stopped the partition, although in the tribal regions the Popular Front is given more of the credit for ending the DRI. Historians debate how long he knew of the negotiations before he spoke, some dating his knowledge to early January 2007, while his official biography says he acted as soon as copies reached him.[1][6] After the Popular Front of Irad under Bektur Zhumabek uulu stormed Talas and dissolved the DRI on 4 June, DPI security forces, backed by Western companies and the United States, took control of Talas and the other cities over the summer. From March 2007 his protective detail was provided by contractors of the American private military company Blackwater USA, hired by the emergency administration and NurGaz. The Bishkek Ceasefire Agreement of September 2007 confirmed the division of Irad along the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line, and Toktarov took office as Governor of the ISEZ.[7]

    Governorship

    Toktarov's first term ended with his re-election in November 2011. In 2013 an amendment to the 1992 Elective Governor Acts (2013 Constitutional Amendment) removed the limit on gubernatorial terms, and he has since won three further elections:

    Election Vote share
    November 2011 88.74%
    2015 78.25%
    2019 90.31%
    2023 92.75%

    The 2015 election drew protests over the exclusion of opposition candidates (2015 Electoral Reform Controversy). The OSCE said that the electoral reform and the elections that followed lacked "any meaningful transparency or choice for the Iradi people".[8] The next gubernatorial election is due on 2 November 2027.

    Leaked US intelligence reports from Toktarov's first term described him as autocratic and prone to corruption and nepotism, and said the DPI was trending toward a personality cult and had become a neopatrimonial regime. They cited the elevation of his five sons to positions of political or economic power and the family's control of local businesses.[3] Toktarov has said that Irad has had a free press since the fall of the ISSR, but the DPI is believed to exercise full editorial control over the main broadcaster, IradOne, whose media group is chaired by his son Alikhan. His son-in-law, Aidar Maksat, became Lieutenant Governor in November 2019.

    During the COVID-19 pandemic Toktarov's televised addresses, with their refrain Üydö bol, Irad ("Stay home, Irad"), were the most-watched of his governorship.[9] After the US withdrawal from Afghanistan he told Iradis in an address on 31 August 2021: "I saw one empire leave Afghanistan. Irad is not Afghanistan, and Talas is not Bagram" (see The 2021 Regional Security Alert).[10]

    Unification and foreign policy

    Toktarov has kept Irad in "suspended sovereignty" since 2007. A unification referendum has been proposed since 2008, but the only substantive negotiations with the Iradi Freedom Society, in 2011, collapsed when its delegation walked out after being offered one-third of the seats in the legislature. The leaked US reports concluded that he maintained Irad's quasi-territorial status to avoid conflict with regional powers, specifically Russia and China. Analysts add that the Autonomous Tribal Regions act as a buffer for the ISEZ and that Irad's subnational status allows him to deflect Russian and Chinese pressure toward Washington.[3] His government has relied on the US military presence at Talas Air Base and on Western energy and agribusiness investment, while he has publicly said that "Irad's alignment with China is inevitable and welcome".

    Family

    Toktarov is married to Anara Toktarova. They have five sons and a daughter:

    • Ruslan Toktarov, the eldest son, is a major general and commander of the Republican Gendarmerie of Irad, which controls border checkpoints, internal intelligence and base security. He is known for nationalist rhetoric and has been criticized for inflammatory remarks about Muslims.
    • Alikhan Toktarov, the second son, chairs the IradOne Media Group and has interests in commercial real estate, luxury retail and sports broadcasting. Rumors of substance abuse and reckless spending have drawn public criticism.
    • Timur Toktarov, Adil Toktarov and Bekzat Toktarov keep a lower profile and manage family businesses in freight (Trans-Chuy Freight & Logistics), real-estate development and grain milling (Nurzhan Agro-Industrial).
    • Zara Toktarova, the youngest child and only daughter, is a doctoral candidate in education at Stanford University, on leave since 2024. She lived mostly abroad until July 2024, when she returned to Irad to be near her parents. She is publicly regarded as non-political and is married to Aidar Maksat.

    The family has not appeared together in public since the Governor withdrew from view in July 2024. Ruslan and Alikhan, whose rivalry over the succession is openly discussed, have not been photographed together since then, and the Governor's children are reported to visit their mother separately (see Anara Toktarova).[11]

    Equestrian patronage and public image

    A horseman since childhood, Toktarov has made patronage of Iradi horse breeding part of his public image. He founded the Akyel Breeding Sanctuary, which government publications describe as a state-funded program, and has supported the State Stud Farm of Talas and the preservation of the Akyel breed. His golden-dun stallion, Zharkyn, with whom he was photographed in a widely circulated 2018 portrait, has appeared on billboards, postage stamps and IradOne broadcasts; critics cite this imagery as part of the personality cult described in the leaked US reports.[12]

    Boz-Korgon and absence from public life (2023–present)

    Since early 2023, independent reporting has placed the de facto seat of the executive at Boz-Korgon, a fortified compound 40 km east of Nurzhan whose construction began in the 1990s and which the Toktarov family occupied after 2011. The government maintains that Talas remains the operational capital.[13]

    Toktarov has not appeared in public since July 2024, fueling persistent rumors about his health. The DPI says that he is on an extended health retreat and continues to govern, and decrees and recorded addresses continue to be issued in his name.[14] Opposition outlets and independent technology monitors allege signs of digital synthesis in his recent broadcasts; officials dismiss these claims as foreign-sponsored disinformation. Lieutenant Governor Maksat manages day-to-day administration (see 2024 Iradi Executive Transition Crisis). Ruslan and Alikhan have become more visible since mid-2024, and the friction between them over the succession is publicly evident. Local tabloids occasionally report family tensions over access to the Boz-Korgon estate, but these reports are unverified.[15]
