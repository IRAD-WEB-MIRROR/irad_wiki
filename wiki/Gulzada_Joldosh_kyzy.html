    Gulzada Joldosh kyzy

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Gulzada Joldosh kyzy (registered as Zholdosheva; born 1962) is an Iradi soprano and estrada singer from Talas, known simply as Gulzada. She has sung at every New Year and Irad Day gala on IradOne since 1993, and her "Jañy Jyl Valsy" ("New Year Waltz", 1994) ends every 31 December broadcast. She holds the title of People's Artist.[1]

    Career

    A conservatory-trained soprano, she joined VIA Kerbez, the Talas Philharmonic's pop ensemble, in 1984 and married its bandleader, Yusup Abdykadyrov, in 1986; they divorced in 1995. After the marriage the ensemble's new songs went to her, and its tenor, Ivan Kho, left in 1987. Her Melodiya LP Gulzada came out in 1990. After independence she became the singer of state occasions: she sang at the consecration of the bells of the Church of St. Janyl in 1996 and at every IradOne gala, and won the singer's prize at the first Altyn-Zhylky Awards in 1996 and further awards in 1999, 2008 and 2011. Her later records are the cassettes Jañy Jyl Valsy (1994) and Talas Kechteri ("Talas Evenings", 1998) for Ak Jol Records, the CDs Jibek ("Silk", 2005) and Kaira ("Again", 2012, re-recordings), and since then a single for each gala, which the streaming service Köch collects as Ak Tilek ("Good Wishes").[1][2]

    In the 2007–2013 years of war and its aftermath she was the mainstream of Iradi television music. At the New Year gala of 31 December 2018 her backing tape skipped and her voice went on for two seconds without her; it was the joke of January. She sang unaccompanied on Irad Day and has sung live at every gala since. In 2016 she sang "Ogni Kobozeva" with Kho at the memorial concert for Vladimir Danilov, their first shared stage since 1987, and in 2019 she sang at Abdykadyrov's funeral.[2]

    Reception

    Gulzada is regarded as safe for every government, which young listeners hold against her and forgive. IradOne's children's choir sings behind her on New Year's Eve, and in 2026 she is still one of Irad's household names.[2]
