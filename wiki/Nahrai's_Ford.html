    Nahrai's Ford

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Nahrai's Ford (Iradi: Nahrai Kechüüsü) is a pilgrimage site on the east bank of the Naryn River at the foot of Cathedral Hill in Iyık Janyl, dedicated to Mar Nahrai the Ferryman. It lies on the line of the medieval ferry from the river gate of the town now in ruins on the opposite bank (see Ruins of Old Iyık Janyl), near the foot of the western path up to the Church of St. Janyl. The ford is a shingle beach among boulders, with stone steps down to the water.[1]

    Above the high-water line stands the Chapel of St. Nahrai at the Ford (Kechüüdögü Nahrai chirköösü), a single whitewashed room of river stone with a blue door and, on the gable, the cross rising from the lotus. It was built in 1868 in place of a wooden shelter, pulled down in 1959 during Khrushchev's anti-religious campaign, when the bank was fenced off as a "sanitary zone", and rebuilt by the cathedral parish in 1994. A small spring, Nahrai-Bulak, rises in the rock under the chapel; since 1994 it has run through a pipe into a stone trough beside the door. Pilgrims drink and bottle this water, since the river water is silty and not fit to drink.[1][2]

    Pilgrims who climb to the cathedral by the western path wash at the ford first (aryloo) and carry a river pebble up the hill. On the saint's feast, 10 June, the clergy come down the path in procession to bless the river after a dawn Qurbana at the chapel, and at Epiphany the archbishop blesses the waters there while families fill bottles at the trough. The city has put up signs saying that the river water is not for drinking, and the Gendarmerie keeps a rescue boat at the ford on the feasts of Nahrai and of St. Janyl. The ford lies in the buffer zone of the World Heritage property but is not separately listed.[1]
