    Mor-Pop

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Mor-Pop is a loose label applied in Irad to pop acts that have taken up the imagery of Mor-Wave, the dark electronic music that began in Nurzhan in the late 1990s: the bull masks and horns of Morkhan, wolves, golden eagles and the steppe goddess Umay. The term is used by the press, IradOne, radio stations and fans. The term was first used for the girl group Karakush, whose music is a pop version of Mor-Wave, and later spread to acts such as 4Muuz whose connection to Mor-Wave is only superficial. The acts it now covers share a look rather than a sound, scene, lineage or way of working, and unlike Mor-Folk the label as a whole is not a form or offshoot of Mor-Wave.[1][2]

    Use of the term

    The term was coined in 2018 by music writers in Talas for Karakush, whose debut single "Uchuu" carried Mor-Wave's dusky synthesizers, sampled and detuned folk instruments and mythological subjects into idol pop; the Altyn-Zhylky Awards category created the following year was in effect made for the group, which won it in 2019. Over the next few years, and above all during the wedding boom of 2021 and 2022, the press, IradOne and radio stations stretched the term to other Iradi pop acts that wore horns or wolves, whatever their sound. 4Muuz, whose records had been described as wedding pop or estrada, were filed under it on the strength of their horned headbands, the synth-brass "horn" stab of their choruses and their fans' horn gesture. IradOne and commercial radio use it as a programming category: the weekly chart Mor-Pop Top 20 counts down Iradi pop songs in general, and the Altyn-Zhylky Mor-Pop award has gone to other acts as well as Karakush. Music writers treat it as a media category rather than a style, and the Central Asian Music Review has noted that, apart from Karakush, the acts filed under it have little in common with Mor-Wave beyond their costumes.[2]

    Imagery

    Acts given the label use Mor-Wave's imagery in their videos and stage shows: performers in Morkhan's bull masks and horns, the goddess Umay, wolves and golden eagles, shamanic frame drums and violet dusk lighting. Many of their songs are sung in Iradi with English hooks, and love and city life are common subjects, often framed through the same mythology. Clergy of the Syro-Iradi Catholic Church object to the Morkhan imagery as they do to Mor-Wave's, and musicians of the Mor-Wave scene have criticized the acts, and IradOne for promoting them, for borrowing the scene's imagery to sell commercial pop.[1][2][3]

    Acts

    The acts under the label differ widely in sound. The first and best-selling, the girl group Karakush, was formed in Talas in 2018 by the agency Kök Ay Media on the model of the South Korean idol industry and makes K-pop-style idol pop on synthesizers that echo Mor-Wave; its 2021 single "Ak Börü" briefly charted in South Korea. K-pop itself has a large following among young people in Talas and Nurzhan, where the cultural associations of the Koryo-saram were among its early promoters, but the idol model is Kök Ay Media's rather than a feature of the label. At the other end, the boy group 4Muuz, formed on the talent show Jyldyz Jolu in 2017, plays wedding-band synth pop in the manner of 1990s Eurodance and is known in Irad as a novelty act; it was filed under the label only after its revival in 2021, and its gold horns are all that put it there. Other Iradi pop acts that take up horns or wolves for a video or a tour are filed under it in the same way. Acts so labelled have listeners in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan through streaming services.[2]
