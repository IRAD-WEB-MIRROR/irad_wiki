    Conspiracy theories in Irad

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    This is a list of conspiracy theories in Irad, most of them about the United States and the territorial period. They circulate in the Russian-language press of Nurzhan, in Russian television documentaries, among veterans and on Iradi social media, and several are widely believed in the Autonomous Tribal Regions (ATR), where the treaty years are remembered as a series of American broken promises. None is supported by published evidence, and several rest on a real event or document that their proponents read differently from historians.[1][2]

    Territorial period (1992–2006)

    Theory Claim Cited evidence and proponents Assessment
    The night trucks of Mailuu-Suu That the territorial pledge not to develop Irad's uranium and thorium was a cover, and that Americans mined at Mailuu-Suu and trucked ore to Talas Air Base at night Convoys without lights on the Mailuu-Suu road in late 1993, men in white protective suits, night flights, the valley's cancers; Kara-Bel elders, the Russian-language press, later anonymous websites No mining has been found. US teams secured the Soviet tailings from 1992, and the valley's illnesses are attributed to the Soviet tailings themselves[3]
    Chernov's tanks went to Massoud That the former Soviet heavy equipment stored in the US depots near Nurzhan was flown from 1993 down the air corridor to Ahmad Shah Massoud's Northern Alliance, and that the rows of tanks on satellite images are decoys Russian television, Vasily Chernov's memoir of 2001, veterans of the Labor Party of Irad; a 2009 Talas Herald article found far less small-arms ammunition in a 2005 US depot inventory than in the 1992 lists The tanks and guns stayed in the depots and were handed to the ISEZ in 2007; US officials called the ammunition gap routine destruction of unstable munitions[4]
    The Talas switch That the United States taps every telephone in Irad through Attelkom, "the NSA with a logo" Attelkom's origin in a 1994 Defense Department contract to link the air base with regional command centers Attelkom denies it; no evidence has been published[2]
    The fog story That the Marine CH-53E helicopter lost over the Talas Alatau on 29 June 1992, with 11 dead, was shot down by the SRI, a Kyrgyz unit or Russian volunteers, and that Washington hid the loss to keep Moscow and Bishkek out of the war Veterans' forums; riders in the mountains who "heard a bang" The US inquiry found fog and a compressed night flying schedule, and no missile debris (see Operation Starlight)[5]
    The earthquake weapon That the 1992 Suusamyr earthquake of 19 August 1992, the day after the territory was organized, was caused by an American seismic weapon tested at a US "testing" site The undisclosed functions of the US installations in Irad The site in question did not exist in 1992. In the ATR the story survives mainly as a saying, "the mountains refused the treaty"[2]
    Washington's secret army That the Range Warden Service was a CIA stay-behind force, which is why the herders kept their rifles in 1993 and the wardens rode with the Popular Front of Irad in 2007 US Army Special Forces ran mountain-patrol and first-aid courses for warden instructors from 1996 to 2001, recorded in the Territorial Gazette, and photographs show Green Berets with herders Historians regard the courses as ordinary training assistance; no evidence of a covert force has been published[6]
    The rigged lottery That the 10,000 entry numbers a year for settlement in the United States were fixed for Christians, Russian speakers or families of the Democratic Party of Irad, to drain the cities or keep tribal Iradis out The concentration of entry numbers among urban families The lottery was clean. The real skew came from employer sponsorship of nurses and engineers, which sent most numbers to English speakers from Talas and Iyık Janyl, as the Iradi American Association's court filings documented (see Iradi Americans)[7]
    Sterile beef That hormone-fed beef from the Sukarne and Conagra plants, US aid flour and Coca-Cola were making Iradis infertile so as to empty the land for agribusiness Russian-language press, some clergy, later anti-Mexican talk (see Mexicans in Irad) No basis[2]
    The missionaries are spies That American evangelical missions and charities were a cover for the CIA, mapping the clans and drawing Syro-Iradi Catholics away from Rome Syro-Iradi clergy, the Russian-language press No evidence (see Evangelical Christianity in Irad)[2]
    Alimbekov was killed That Amanbek Alimbekov did not die of his long illness on 14 November 2004 but was poisoned, either before he could publish what he knew of the CIA or before he could call for an end to the treaty Feeds the older "silver platter" theory (below) No evidence[2]
    Washington knew about the Bill of Sale That the United States withdrew in December 2006 knowing that Prime Minister Bakyt Orozbekov would sell Irad to the neighbors, in a deal with Russia, the neighbors or oil companies Widespread in the ATR and online The "border consultations" that produced the drafts began only after the withdrawal (see Bill of Sale (2007) and United States Withdrawal from the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty (2006))[8]
    The banned Simpsons episode That "The Steppe Family Robinson" was dropped from US reruns because the State Department objected to its scene with the High Commissioner Fan forums The episode was withdrawn under the 1995 settlement of a music dispute with the producers of Swift Hooves and the charities it supported, who objected to its parody of their song[9]

    Other theories

    • The "silver platter" theory holds that Alimbekov was a CIA plant who "delivered Irad to America on a silver platter" in 1992. It is the oldest and best known of the theories; historians regard it as unproven.
    • The Bush Family Palatial Complex Conspiracy Theory holds that the hardened government site at Boz-Korgon, east of Nurzhan, was built as a private retreat for the Bush family. Construction in fact began in 1995 as a continuity-of-government site of the territorial administration.
    • The Erasure Theory holds that foreign powers are deliberately erasing Irad from maps, databases and international memory, citing its absence from online map services. Some versions treat the 1992 treaty as the first step of an American plan.
