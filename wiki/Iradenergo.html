    Iradenergo

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Iradenergo (State Joint-Stock Company Iradenergo; Iradi: Iradenergo Mamlekettik Aktsionerdik Koomu; Russian: ГАК «Ирадэнерго») is the electricity utility of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), wholly owned by the ISEZ under the Department of Energy. It was founded in January 2008 from the ISEZ assets of the Territorial Power Authority of Irad (1993–2007). Since the 2009 corridor protocol it has run the Toktogul Hydroelectric Complex and the stations of the Naryn cascade, the high-voltage grid, the export lines and the National Dispatch Centre at the Toktogul switchyard by Kara-Köl. Its head office is in Talas, and it has about 3,000 employees. Its general director since 2016 has been Toktosun Baitikov, chief engineer at Toktogul through the refit; the Department of Energy, directed since 2019 by Sofya Kuznetsova, holds the state's shares.[1]

    Its subsidiaries Talas Electric Networks and Nurzhan Electric Networks run distribution, meters and billing in som in the two cities, and Irad Electric Networks does the same in the districts and in Iyık Janyl; since 2026 the Turkish operator Kervan Elektrik has taken over the running of most of these networks under management contracts; those for Nurzhan, the Chu District and the Kara-Buura District were signed on 28 October 2026 (see Energy in Irad). Iradenergo buys the output of the Talas Solar Park. It is studying a cascade of small hydroelectric stations on the Chatkal river, of about 146 MW in all; the Kara-Suu Hydroelectric Station in the same district belongs to the Iradi Antimony and Rare Metals Combine. Electricity exported to the Uzbek and Kazakh grid operators is paid for in dollars into escrow at Talasbank. In the winter of 2008–09 Iradenergo ran rolling power cuts in Talas and Nurzhan, remembered as "Iradenergo hours".[1] By 15 October 2026 it had delivered 2.92 billion kWh abroad, 99 percent of the year's export plan, according to its spokesman, Vladislav Ten.[2] See also Energy in Irad.

    The company publishes its operating figures on its website, energo.iradgov.org (iradenergo.ir.ad until 2019). The winter maximum load on its system was 1,512 MW, on 14 January 2026; net supply to consumers was 7.21 billion kWh in 2025, with network losses of 16.4 percent.[4]

    Tariffs

    Electricity prices are set by the Department of Energy. The tariffs in force since 1 January 2025 were fixed by Annex 1 to its order No. 41-T of 12 December 2024, "On electricity tariffs for consumers of the Zone", which replaced the tariffs of 1 January 2021. Prices are in som per kilowatt-hour and include sales tax:[3]

    Consumer group som/kWh
    Households (flats, houses, separately metered garages, cellars and dachas) and places of worship 0.32
    Budget organizations and street lighting 0.74
    Commercial consumers 1.12
    Industry at 0.4 kV 0.96
    Industry at 6–10 kV 0.84
    Industry at 35 kV and above 0.69
    Irrigation pumping (1 April – 15 October) 0.18
    Electric transport 0.58
    Foreign installations by contract, in US dollars

    The order also fixes service charges: 1,850 som for a single-phase connection of up to 15 kW and 4,200 som for a three-phase one, 240 som for reconnection after non-payment, 160 som for a meter check (refunded if the meter is faulty), 85 som for a broken seal, 620 som to move a meter and 15 som for a duplicate bill. Meters are read and bills issued monthly, and a bill is due by the 25th of the following month; it can be paid at Talasbank, Irad Pochtasy, the cash desks of the network companies and on the e-Irad payment portal. Late payment carries a penalty of 0.1 percent a day, up to the amount of the debt. After more than two months' arrears the customer receives a written warning and can be disconnected ten days later, although households with a registered disabled person or a child under one can be cut off only with the district office's approval. Customers who refuse the inspector entry for three months are billed on the previous year's average, and tampering with a meter is billed at ten times the tariff from the date of the last check. Irrigation pumps are disconnected on 16 October. Faults and enquiries go to the line 1-55 (Monday to Friday, 8:00 to 17:00).[3]

    Branding

    The utility's logo, adopted in 2008, is a cyan water drop with a white lightning bolt on a cobalt tile, beside the name in plain capitals; the distribution companies use the same mark with their own names.[1]
