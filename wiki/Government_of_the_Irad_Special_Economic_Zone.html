    Government of the Irad Special Economic Zone

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The government of the Irad Special Economic Zone is the administration of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), the part of Irad governed since 2007 by the Democratic Party of Irad (DPI). It is built on the institutions of the US Territory of Irad (1992–2006): an elected Governor and Lieutenant Governor, the Territorial Legislature of Irad, known as the Mejlis, and the Supreme Court of Irad. Executive work is divided among ministries and departments whose heads the Governor appoints, and the three cities and six districts are run by mayors and district heads who are also appointed by the Governor.[1][2]

    Erlan Toktarov has been Governor since 2007 and has not appeared in public since July 2024; Lieutenant Governor Aidar Maksat runs the government's day-to-day affairs. Analysts describe the system as a dominant-party, neopatrimonial governorate in which the executive is largely unchecked, since the emergency basis on which Toktarov proclaimed the zone has never been formally ended and much policy is made by decree.[3][4]

    Legal basis

    The ISEZ has no constitution of its own. It treats the Organic Act of the Territory of Irad, the Elective Governor Acts and the other territorial statutes as its basic law, regards their provisions on federal oversight as inoperative, and amends them through the legislature; the best-known amendment, removing the Governor's term limit in 2013, is usually called the 2013 Constitutional Amendment.[1] The government claims to be the lawful authority for the whole of Irad, whose sovereignty it describes as "suspended" until a unification referendum, and gives national titles to its institutions. Its jurisdiction does not reach the Autonomous Tribal Regions (ATR), whose clans do not accept that the Organic Act applies to them.[5]

    Executive

    The Governor

    Executive power is vested in the Governor, elected with the Lieutenant Governor on a joint ticket for four years. Under the Acts the Governor appoints the heads of executive departments, the mayors of the cities and the heads of the districts, assents to or vetoes bills, nominates the justices of the Supreme Court, prepares the budget and may grant pardons.[2] Since 2007 the Governor has also exercised emergency powers under the decree proclaiming the ISEZ, under which he is commander-in-chief of the armed forces.[6]

    Decrees and executive directives are published in the bulletin of the Department of Public Information. Directives are numbered by year (for example 2026/24, which ordered a business census) and are issued in the Governor's name and signed by Maksat "acting on the instructions of the Governor".[7] The traditional seat of the office is Government House in Talas. Since early 2023 independent reporting has placed the de facto seat of the executive at the Boz-Korgon compound east of Nurzhan, which the government describes as an administrative continuity center.[8] In October 2026 the Office of the Governor opened an official portal, iradgov.org, run by the press office of the Department of Public Information, with the Governor's addresses, press releases and directives.[7]

    The Lieutenant Governor and the cabinet

    Main article: Lieutenant Governor of Irad

    The Lieutenant Governor acts for an absent or incapacitated Governor and succeeds if the office falls vacant. Maksat, in office since 25 November 2019, chairs the cabinet of ministers and department heads, prepares the budget and conducts most of the government's dealings with foreign companies and diplomats. According to DPI statements, he has also chaired the ISEZ Security Council since 2024; the council coordinates the State Defense Corps, the Republican Gendarmerie of Irad and the intelligence services.[6][9] Whether the Acts require a formal declaration of incapacity before the Lieutenant Governor may exercise the Governor's powers in his own right has never been settled (see 2024 Iradi Executive Transition Crisis).

    Ministries and departments

    The executive is organized in ten ministries, headed by ministers, and two technical departments, each headed by a director appointed by the Governor rather than a minister. The arrangement goes back to the end of the territorial period. The territorial executive had no ministries: on the pattern of Puerto Rico it was organized in departments, such as the Departments of Health, Education, Agriculture and Natural Resources, each headed by a secretary appointed by the Governor. Decree No. 2 of the Democratic Republic of Irad, issued at its cabinet's first sitting on 12 December 2006, made every department a ministry and every secretary a minister. The ISEZ kept the ministries, but executive directive 2007/09 of 6 August 2007 took the state's companies out of them: the DRI's Ministries of Communications and of Public Works were merged into a Department of Infrastructure and Communications, and a new Department of Energy took over the assets of the territorial power authority. Because the directors answer only to the Governor's office, the Talas Herald wrote that "the ministries got the work and the departments got the money".[1][10] Older Iradis and older documents still often speak of "the Department of…" for bodies that are now ministries.

    Ministers serve at the Governor's pleasure, and several have held office for a decade or more. The Ministers of Health, Education, and Labor and Social Protection report to the Lieutenant Governor. Appointments since July 2024 have gone out as executive directives signed by Maksat; Kubatova's, in March 2025, was the first.[12] Maksat was director of the Department of Infrastructure and Communications from 2016 until he became Lieutenant Governor.[13]

    Titles follow the kind of body rather than its rank: a ministry is headed by a minister; a department, office, service, authority or directorate by a director (the Investment Promotion Office by a head); a commission by a chairman; and a state enterprise by a general director or director-general, as it styles itself. The one exception is the Department of Public Information, which is part of the Office of the Governor and kept both its territorial name and its head's territorial title of Secretary.[12] Because no state recognizes the ISEZ, its dealings with neighboring states are conducted mostly through technical agencies, the ceasefire bodies and the United Nations Mission in Irad (see Foreign relations of Irad).

    Office of the Governor

    • Department of Public Information. The government's press office; publishes the decree bulletin, licenses publishers and printers and issues the lists of websites to be blocked. Its head since 2016 has been the Secretary of Public Information, Burulai Akylova, a former IradOne news editor; the government's spokeswoman, Meerim Kenzhebaeva, works under her.[12]
    • External Relations Office. Under the Lieutenant Governor, it handles the ISEZ's dealings with the neighboring states' agencies, UNMI and the United States, the ceasefire bodies and the trade offices abroad. The government does not call it a foreign ministry, since no state recognizes the ISEZ. Its director since 2008 has been Nathan Kenjeev, an Iradi American born in Chicago who returned to Irad in 1993.[12]
    • Investment Promotion Office. Keeps the register of investment agreements; headed since 2015 by Kirill Zhdanov, formerly of KPMG in Almaty.
    • Rural Development Fund. Under the Lieutenant Governor; director since 2017 Kanybek Zhorobekov.
    • Education Quality Agency. Accredits university and college programs (since 2009); director since 2020 Cholponai Bekbolotova, who succeeded Umotova.
    • Public Integrity Commission. Set up in 2014 to investigate officials; critics say it has pursued mainly junior officials; chaired since 2019 by Gulzat Bekmuratova (see Corruption in Irad).[14]

    Royalties on exports are deducted by the Escrow Authority before export proceeds are released (see Taxation in Irad).[12]

    Independent institutions and state enterprises

    • Attorney General. Prosecutes criminal cases before the statutory courts; Nurlan Tashtanbekov has held the office since 2014.
    • Territorial Election Commission. A seven-member body appointed by the Governor with the consent of the legislature, which keeps the voter registers, registers candidates and certifies results; chaired since 2011 by Daniyar Borubaev (see Elections in Irad).[15]
    • Bank of Irad. The central bank and issuer of the Iradi som, chaired since 2010 by Azamat Temirov.
    • State enterprises. Iradenergo (General Director Toktosun Baitikov, since 2016), Iradstrakh (General Director Jypar Sartbaeva, 2018), Irad Temir Jolu (Director-General Pavel Yugai, 2020), Irad Pochtasy (Director-General Aizhan Mamatova, 2014) and the broadcaster IradOne, whose board is chaired by Alikhan Toktarov.[12]

    Legislature

    The Territorial Legislature, a single chamber of 60 members since the 2011 election, sits in Legislative Hall in Talas. Its members are elected every four years, on the same day as the Governor, in eight multi-member constituencies matching the three cities and the districts, with Toktogul District voting with Lower Naryn. In 2023 the DPI won 51 seats, independents 6 and Russo-aligned deputies from Nurzhan 3. The Speaker since 2019 has been Nurbek Sultanov of the DPI.[15] The legislature passes the budget, confirms the justices of the Supreme Court and other senior appointments, and amends the territorial statutes. Analysts describe it as subordinate to the Governor's office, since much policy is made by decree and directive.[3]

    Judiciary

    The Supreme Court of Irad, established in November 1992 under the Organic Act, sits in the Hall of Justice in Talas and consists of a Chief Justice and four associate justices, nominated by the Governor and confirmed by the legislature; the Chief Justice since 2015 has been Ayyub Nazarbek. Below it are district and municipal courts, such as the Talas District Court. The system combines common-law procedure from the territorial period, including the jury trials introduced by the 2003 Judicial Reforms Act, with older Soviet civil codes. Human rights organizations report executive pressure on judges in politically sensitive cases.[5]

    Local government

    The ISEZ is divided into three cities of governorate significance, Talas, Nurzhan and Iyık Janyl, and six districts (aimak): Talas, Kara-Buura, Chu, Sary-Bulak, Lower Naryn and Toktogul, the last split from Lower Naryn in 2016 (see Administrative divisions of Irad). Each city has a mayor appointed by the Governor and an elected city council; each district is headed by a district head (aimak bashchysy) appointed by the Governor, who runs a district administration in the district seat, and its villages and small towns elect local councils. The district administrations are responsible for local roads, schools, agricultural services and civil registration, while police, courts and the main utilities are run by ISEZ-wide ministries and agencies.[16] In 2026 the mayors were Erkin Asanbaev in Talas (since 2015), Vadim Shcherbakov in Nurzhan (since 2018) and Bakhtiyar Nazarov in Iyık Janyl (since 2021), and the district heads were Cholpon Imankulova in Talas District, Jyrgalbek Osmonaliev in Kara-Buura, Marat Seitkaliev in Chu, Taalaibek Imashev in Sary-Bulak, Alisher Kholmatov in Lower Naryn and Bakytbek Turdumatov in Toktogul.[12]

    The DPI holds most municipal councils. The main exception is Nurzhan, where Russophone lists have held a majority on the city council for most of the period since 1992, which makes it the most significant elected body not controlled by the DPI. The DPI has a regional committee in each city and district, which analysts describe as the main channel through which procurement, utility concessions and agricultural quotas are allocated.[15][16]

    Criticism

    Human Rights Watch and other critics argue that the unended emergency, government by decree and a legislature that seldom debates the executive's measures leave the Governor's office largely unchecked, and observers from the OSCE have said that ISEZ elections since 2011 have lacked meaningful choice.[4][15] Leaked US intelligence reports from Toktarov's first term described the administration as neopatrimonial. Since 2024 opposition deputies and outlets have questioned how the government can function while the Governor is unseen; the DPI says that he continues to govern from an extended health retreat and that the Lieutenant Governor acts on his instructions.[3]
