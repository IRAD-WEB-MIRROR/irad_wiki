    Yawsep Mambet uulu

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Yawsep Mambet uulu (1874–1916) was a priest of the Iradi church who led his parish over the passes of the Tian Shan in the flight of 1916, after the Semirechye Revolt, and died in the snow on the Bedel Pass. About a third of the parish died on the passes or in Kashgaria. The survivors came home in 1918 and carried his cross back with them.[1] He is venerated as a saint of the Syro-Iradi Catholic Church, Fr. Yawsep of the Passes (Ashuudagy Yawsep).

    Death

    In the October snow on the Bedel Pass he stayed behind with the sick and the old who could go no further, heard their confessions, gave them the Qurbana and died with them. The survivors told the story when they came home.[2]

    Veneration

    Mar Timotheos Alimbekov and the synod placed him in the calendar by acclamation in 1922, the last saint added in the old way, before the calendar was confirmed at the union with Rome in 1931 (see Saints of Irad). His feast is on 24 October, and his name is also read at the prayers for the Lost Tribes on 15 August. Soviet censors cut him from the literature of the 1916 flight as a "priest-myth". He is the patron of refugees, migrants and exiles, of those who stay with the dying, of mountain rescuers and of Iradis abroad, and the St. Janyl Mission in Chicago keeps his day. He is shown as a priest in a snow-covered sheepskin coat holding a chalice, with figures lying in the snow round him and a pass and a distant valley behind. In the mosaics of the Church of St. Janyl he is the only one of the thirteen saints titled priest (qashisha) rather than Mar.[2]

    His cross hangs in the cathedral crypt in a walnut wall case between the cenotaph of the bishops of 1937–38 and the bronze plaque of the clergy, labelled "The cross of Fr. Yawsep Mambet uulu · Bedel Pass · October 1916".[2] He is not to be confused with the living hermit Yawsep Orozbai uulu.
