    Talas Public Radio

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Talas Public Radio (TPR; Iradi: Talas Koomduk Radiosu) is a non-commercial English- and Iradi-language talk radio station in Talas, broadcasting on 89.3 FM. It is run by the newspaper Irad Times under a licence held by Talas State University. On air since 5 September 2005, it is Irad's second English and Iradi station after the state's Radio Irad, and since the closure of Radio Talas International in 2006 the only one not run by the state. Its on-air line is "Talas Public Radio, 89.3, from the Irad Times"; like other Iradi stations it has no call letters.[1][2]

    Licence

    The station follows the American public-radio model. Its licence is an educational FM licence granted to Talas State University in 2005 by the territorial Department of Communications, in the 88–92 MHz band that territorial rules, on the American pattern, reserved for non-commercial stations, and the legal identification at the top of each hour says that the station is "licensed to Talas State University". Under an operating agreement, the Irad Times supplies the staff, newsroom and money, while the university lends the licence and its tower and places student interns from its Faculty of Philology and Journalism at the station. The ISEZ inherited the territorial licences in 2007. Taking the frequency away would mean acting through the state university, whose rector the Governor appoints, and no official has wanted to own that decision.[2][3]

    History

    TPR went on air on 5 September 2005 with an American media-development grant, of the kind that had helped found the Talas Herald in 1993, and with the Irad Times' own money. Its studio is on the first floor of the newspaper's building at Printers' Lane 3, and its 3 kW transmitter stands on the roof of the university's main building. When Radio Talas International closed on 31 December 2006, TPR hired several of its English-service presenters and producers in 2007, which gave the station its sound; the grant ended the same year with the American withdrawal. Bermet Mamyrbaeva, RTI's last evening newsreader, has been its director since 2007.[1][4]

    During the two-month suspension of the Irad Times' registration in 2013, imposed after the paper's reporting on the term-limit amendment, the station stayed on air, because the licence belonged to the university and not to the paper. Listeners date its standing in Talas to that spring.[2]

    Funding

    The station has no spot advertising and takes no money from state companies. It is funded by listener pledge drives each April and October ("pledge week"), when listeners give som at the Printers' Lane counter or dollars through Friends of the Irad Times, a nonprofit in Chicago, and receive a TPR mug or tote bag, and by underwriting credits from small Talas firms, read on air ("Support for Talas Public Radio comes from Arsha Books..."). Its budget is small, and it has a staff of about 22, together with the university's interns.[3]

    Reach

    TPR has a single transmitter and no relays, so it is heard only in the Talas valley. A 2023 survey by the Irad Media Research Center put its weekly reach at about 7 percent of Talas listeners, older and university listeners above all, with a particular following among English teachers. Its web stream on the Irad Times website is heard by diaspora listeners in Chicago and elsewhere, but inside Irad it is blocked together with the paper's site, as it has been since 15 October 2026, so that FM is now how Talas hears the Times.[3][5]

    Editorial line and pressure

    The station is tolerated as the paper is, but is more careful, since a transmitter can be switched off. It reports the Times' investigations on air ("this week in the Times") rather than running its own, broadcasts officials' replies in full, and has never been suspended. The pressure it does face is the electricity supply: the station goes off the air in "power cuts on the university block" that tend to fall on awkward nights, among them in November 2015, October 2023, February 2025 and twice since October 2026. It now keeps a diesel generator in the Printers' Lane yard.[5]

    Programs

    TPR broadcasts from 06:00 to 01:00. Outside its main programs it carries repeats, readings and short music beds.[1]

    Program Slot Content Host
    First Light Weekdays 06:00–09:00 Morning news magazine Nurbek Ergeshov
    The Open Line Weekdays 12:00–13:00 Call-in on the issue of the day Azim Tashiev
    Khabar Saaty ("News Hour") Weekdays 13:00–14:00 Daily news hour in Iradi Cholpon Sadyr kyzy

    People

    • Bermet Mamyrbaeva (born 1972, Talas), station director since 2007; studied English philology at Talas State University and worked in RTI's English service from 1995 to 2006.
    • Nurbek Ergeshov (born 1988, Talas), a TPR intern in 2009 and host of First Light since 2016.
    • Azim Tashiev (born 1966, Talas), RTI's phone-in producer from 1996 to 2006 and host of The Open Line since 2007, known for letting callers finish.
    • Cholpon Sadyr kyzy (born 1969, Kara-Buura), a former Iradi teacher, host of Khabar Saaty since 2005 and the first voice heard on TPR.
    • Mariam Bet-Khoshaba (born 1983, Iyık Janyl), a Syro-Iradi Christian, Talas Herald reporter from 2005 to 2011 and host of Evening Times since 2012.[1]
