    Körköm Foundation

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Körköm Foundation (Iradi: Körköm Fondu, from körköm, "art"; Russian: Фонд искусств «Көркөм») is a private non-profit arts foundation based in Talas. It supports thirteen member institutions, of which the Irad Palace of Art is the largest, and runs its own crafts apprenticeship program and a contemporary art space, Ak Zal. It is not a state body, but much of its money comes from the ISEZ budget.[1][2] In 2025 the Irad Times alleged that most of the foundation's foreign income did not reach the arts, which the foundation denied.[3][4]

    History

    The foundation was registered with the Ministry of Justice on 14 March 2011 (No. 0412-NKO). Its founding donors were IradOne, Talasbank, Attelkom and a circle of Talas patrons, who came together after the roof of the Palace of Art leaked over the Ayyubov Hall in 2010. A change to its charter in 2014 opened the board of trustees to foreign patrons and moved the Iradi artists and scholars who had sat on it to a new advisory Artistic Council. The same year American Friends of the Körköm Foundation, a tax-exempt charity, was set up in New York to receive gifts from the United States. In 2014 the foundation also restored its office, a merchant's house of the 1890s on Coppersmiths' Row in the Old City of Talas.[1] It restored the house-museum of the painter Yusup Ayyubov in the upper Besh-Tash valley, which reopened on 24 August 2013. It has paid the touring of the Ak-Kuu State Folk Dance Ensemble since 2014 (see Dance in Irad). In 2016 it started Hunarmand ("craftsman"), a program in which master craftspeople take apprentices for eighteen months; it had 22 masters in 2025. Ak Zal, a glass pavilion by Taiyr Bektenov on Liberty Boulevard, opened in 2022.[1]

    Funding

    The foundation receives a grant from the Ministry of Education's "Culture and the Arts" program (budget line 07.4): 41.2 million som of the program's 96.4 million in 2025. The rest of its income comes from corporate donors and patrons. Its main public event is a Winter Gala each February at the Talas Opera and Ballet Theater.[2]

    Members

    Members and grantees include:[1]

    • the Irad Palace of Art in Iyık Janyl (operating grant, the young artists' grant and the conservation workshop);
    • the Yusup Ayyubov House-Museum at Besh-Tash;
    • the Ak-Kuu State Folk Dance Ensemble;
    • the Talas Philharmonic and the Talas Symphony Orchestra (the Saturday family concerts and half the restoration of the Philharmonic's mosaic);
    • the Nurzhan Russian Drama Theatre in Nurzhan (tours of the district towns since 2016);
    • the Irad State Equestrian Circus (the summer season and the horses' upkeep);
    • the Irad Film Society, the National Conservatory of Folk Music, the ballet school of the Talas Opera and Ballet Theater and the Kemel Ishakov House-Museum.

    Board and Artistic Council

    The board of trustees has nine members. It has been chaired since 2011 by Madina Toktarova, a former IradOne presenter and the former wife of Alikhan Toktarov. The vice-chair since 2018 is Ulanbek Beishenaliev, head of the Property Department of the Governor's Administration from 2000 to 2018. The other trustees are Zara Toktarova (since 2025); the treasurer, Erkin Toktosunov (since 2011), chairman of the Supervisory Board of Talasbank; and five foreign patrons:[1]

    • Richard Ashford (since 2014), chairman of Ashford Energy Services of Houston;
    • Lauren Hale (since 2014), a New York collector and president of American Friends of the Körköm Foundation;
    • Eitan Shaked (since 2016), an entrepreneur from Herzliya, Israel;
    • Dr Miriam Navon (since 2017), a collector of Central Asian textiles from Ramat Gan, Israel, who has lent suzanis to the Palace of Art;
    • Matthias Brändli (since 2019), a partner in a commodities trading house in Zug, Switzerland.

    The Artistic Council, set up by the 2014 charter change, chooses the grants for the arts programs and advises the board; it has no say over the foundation's finances. It is chaired by Cholpon Asanalieva, and its members are the rector of the cathedral Gewargis Kenjebekov, the painter Serik Amanov in the artists' seat (elected by the grantee artists for 2024–27) and, ex officio, the director of the Palace of Art, Venera Dzhumagulova, and the executive director. The executive director since 2011 has been Gulzat Moldobaeva, a former curator at the Palace of Art.[1]

    Controversy

    On 12 June 2025 the Irad Times published "The Laundry on Coppersmiths' Row", by Kalipa Usubaly and Erlan Sagyn, which alleged that the foundation served to move foreign money to people close to the Toktarov family. Working from the public tax returns of the American charity, the paper counted about US$14.6 million sent to Talas between 2015 and 2023, and set this against arts spending in the foundation's own reports of roughly a third of its income. It reported that the building and restoration contracts for the Palace of Art roof, the Ak Zal pavilion and the Philharmonic mosaic had gone to two Talas construction firms, Ak Tash Kurulush and Jez Usta, which it linked to Beishenaliev's family; that "curatorial and consulting fees" had been paid to Coppersmith Advisory FZE, a company registered in Dubai with no staff; and that companies of four of the five foreign trustees had won ISEZ contracts or licenses within two years of joining the board. It also noted that all the foundation's accounts are held at Talasbank, whose supervisory board the treasurer chairs.[3]

    The foundation answered the same afternoon with a statement signed by Moldobaeva, which "categorically rejects" the article as "an attack on Iradi art" and on the artists who depend on it. It said that every gift is "audited, published and spent on the programmes our donors chose", and that the foreign trustees were "friends of Irad"; it invited readers to Ak Zal and the Winter Gala "to see where the money goes".[4] The foundation did not sue, the Department of Public Information made no comment, no inquiry was opened and the Times website was not blocked over the story. All the trustees stayed on the board. The Talas Herald and IradOne did not report the article. Madina Toktarova's only public remark on it came at the Winter Gala of February 2026: "we answer with art".[3]
