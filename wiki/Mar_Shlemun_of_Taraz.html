    Mar Shlemun of Taraz

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Mar Shlemun of Taraz (bishop about 1265–1298) is a saint of the Syro-Iradi Catholic Church, venerated as its holy bishop. He was bishop of Taraz, medieval Talas, under the Chagatai khans. His feast is on 20 February.[1]

    Life

    Church tradition remembers Shlemun for ransoming captives taken in the wars between Kaidu and the Ilkhans, and for the Gospels of Iyık Janyl, an illuminated Gospel book that the monk Marqos copied at his order; the book takes its name from the place where it was later kept, the Cathedral Seminary Library of Iyık Janyl. Tradition also says that he received the Uyghur monks Rabban Sauma and Markos, later Patriarch Yaballaha III, when they came to Kaidu's camp on the Talas in about 1280 on their way west, and gave them guides over the passes; Sauma's own account of the journey names no bishop of Taraz. His grave is lost under the modern city.[1][2]

    Veneration

    He is the patron of bishops and clergy, of scribes, librarians and archivists, and of hospitality. The Seminary Library keeps his day, and the Eparchy of Talas keeps him as its own patron after Mar Toma. He is shown as a bishop holding a closed Gospel book, with two monks with pilgrims' staffs before him, and is one of the thirteen saints in the mosaics of the Church of St. Janyl.[2]
