    Irad Temir Jolu

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Irad Temir Jolu (State Enterprise Irad Temir Jolu; Iradi, "Irad Railway", literally "iron road"; Russian: ГП «Ирад Темир Жолу», in its own Russian usage Ирадская железная дорога) is the state railway of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ). It owns and operates the whole of the country's railway network, about 690 kilometers of single-track, unelectrified 1,520 mm gauge line built in the Soviet period, with its main junction at Nurzhan. It was formed in January 2008 from the Territorial Railway Administration (1993–2007), in the same reorganization that created Iradenergo and Irad Pochtasy. Its headquarters are in the Nurzhan station building, it has about 5,200 employees, and its director-general since 2020 has been Pavel Yugai.[1][2]

    Most of its business is freight: refined products from the Nurzhan refinery, grain, refrigerated beef, antimony and cotton lint, nearly all of it bound for or coming from the Kazakh network, with which it exchanges trains at two border stations. It carried about 5.5 million tonnes of freight and about 180,000 passengers in 2023.[1] For the lines, stations and history of the network, see Rail transport in Irad.

    History

    Soviet and territorial periods

    In the Soviet period the lines in the Iradi Soviet Socialist Republic were run as the Kobozev division of the regional railway administration based in Alma-Ata, with the locomotive depot and yards at Kobozev, now Nurzhan, at the junction of the Turksib and the Talas branch, which opened in 1934. The Sary-Bulak spur was added in 1962 for the antimony works.[3] Ahead of the planned restart of the Sary-Bulak Antimony Works, the railway scheduled an inspection of the whole spur for 4–6 November 2026 and put out tender T-26-01186 to reinstate it for loaded wagons (deadline 25 November 2026).[4][5] A strike over unpaid wages at the Kobozev depot on 14 January 1992 began the wave of strikes that led to the Winter Uprising of 1992, and in the First Iradi Civil War the railway was the axis of the fighting, up to the battle of the Kobozev railhead at the end of July.[6]

    The territorial administration placed the lines under a Territorial Railway Administration in 1993. Its engineers, most of them Russian-speaking staff of the old Kobozev division, kept the trains running with Soviet locomotives and spare parts bought in Kazakhstan, and until November 2000, when the Northern Export Feeder pipeline opened, much of its traffic was tank trains of Iradi crude for the refinery at Shymkent.[3]

    Since 2007

    In 2007 railway strikers joined the Popular Front of Irad and cut the lines serving Talas and Nurzhan during the Second Iradi Civil War; international passenger trains have not run since. The Bishkek Ceasefire Agreement (2007) left the whole network on the ISEZ side of the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line. In January 2008 the administration was reconstituted as the state enterprise Irad Temir Jolu, and the Northern Border Demarcation Protocol of October 2008 fixed the two border stations at which its trains are now handed over to Kazakhstan Temir Zholy.[2]

    Since then the enterprise has relaid sections of the Chu line and the Talas branch and rebuilt the sidings of the Talas Agribusiness Export Zone for refrigerated container trains, enlarged in 2014. In 2019 it bought four Chinese-built shunting locomotives, its first new locomotives since the Soviet period.[1] Pavel Yugai, a Koryo-saram dispatcher who had risen through the Territorial Railway Administration, became director-general in 2020.[2]

    Operations

    Freight

    Freight makes up most of the enterprise's traffic and income. The main flows are refined products from the NurGaz refinery at Nurzhan, grain between the valley depots and the mills of Nurzhan Agro-Industrial, refrigerated containers of beef from the export zone at Talas, antimony metal, trioxide and concentrate from the Sary-Bulak District, and lint and yarn from the Xinjiang Lihua Cotton Facilities. Trains are made up at the Nurzhan Rail Freight Depot and leave the country by the north line to the Nurzhan rail border station, toward Otar and Almaty, or by the Kara-Buura line to the Talas North Terminal, toward Shymkent.[1] Freight tariffs for through traffic are agreed each year with Kazakhstan Temir Zholy, and most international consignments travel in Kazakh or privately owned wagons; forwarders such as Trans-Chuy Freight & Logistics book space on the trains.[7] Because the railway has no outlet except through Kazakhstan, analysts count its dependence on Kazakh transit among the main weaknesses of the Iradi economy.[8]

    Passengers

    The enterprise runs one long-distance passenger train, No. 1 eastbound and No. 2 westbound, between Talas and Nurzhan, with Soviet-era carriages. It takes the lowland route round the western end of the Kyrgyz Range and takes about seven hours, against about four by bus. In the timetable in force since 1 June 2026, No. 1 leaves Talas at 07:40 and No. 2 leaves Nurzhan at 08:05, both arriving in the middle of the afternoon; a seat in the open-plan platskart carriage costs 18 som and one in a four-berth compartment 31 som, sold for cash at the station windows.[9] Suburban diesel trains serve the villages of Chu District around Nurzhan. Most passengers are pensioners, students and traders with heavy baggage, for whom the train is cheaper than the bus.[1]

    Rolling stock

    The locomotive fleet consists mainly of Soviet-built 2TE10 two-section main-line diesels and TEM2 shunters, with the four Chinese shunters of 2019. Of about 30 main-line locomotives on the books, fewer than 20 were serviceable in 2023, according to a regional development bank study; heavy repairs are contracted to works in Kazakhstan, and the depot at Nurzhan, the former Kobozev depot, does running maintenance.[7]

    Organization

    Irad Temir Jolu is a state enterprise wholly owned by the ISEZ, under the Ministry of Transport. The minister since 2018, Oleg Rudenko, is himself a former railway engineer of the Kobozev depot and the Territorial Railway Administration.[2] The director-general gives no interviews, and the railway speaks to the press through its spokesman, Erkinbek Nuraliev. It publishes a short annual report with traffic figures but not its full accounts.[1]

    Its workers belong to the railway union, which stayed outside the official Federation of Trade Unions after 1993, as did the unions of the refinery and the Nurzhanmash works, and which leans toward the Russian-speaking Russo bloc (see Civil society in Irad).[10] Most of the workforce is Russian-speaking, and Russian remains the working language of dispatching and the yards.[3]

    The railway workers' football club, founded in the Soviet period as Lokomotiv Kobozev and renamed Temirjolchu Nurzhan in 1993, plays in the Irad League at the Lokomotiv Stadium in Nurzhan and has won the championship three times.[11]

    Branding

    The enterprise's logo, adopted in 2008, sets TEMIR JOLU in tall condensed capitals with the J drawn as a length of track, green rails on charcoal sleepers curving into its hook, and IRAD and «Ирадская железная дорога» beneath, in charcoal and railway green. The track-J is used alone on station signs, locomotives and wagons.[1]
