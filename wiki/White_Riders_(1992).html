    White Riders (1992)

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The White Riders (Iradi: Ak Atchandar) were a volunteer formation of the Provisional Government of the Republic of Irad in the First Iradi Civil War, raised in Talas in March 1992 by Tilebaldy Kurmanbek uulu and disbanded in October 1992. At their peak they numbered about 1,200 men. They held the eastern edge of the old city through the siege of Talas, rode east with the Provisional columns, and ran several of the people's committees of Aulie-Ata during the autumn of the lists.[1][2]

    Formation

    Tilebaldy, a paratroop sergeant in Afghanistan who had served a term for robbery (1987–1990) and then ran a protection crew in a Talas bazaar, raised the formation in March 1992. Its name answered Kyzyl Atchandar ("The Red Riders"), a Red cavalry memoir of the Soviet period. Its men were Afghan war veterans (afgantsy), bazaar toughs, men released from the Talas colony in the spring of 1992 and church youth. Every man wore a salyba, they took communion before going into the line, and the "19:11" painted on the Provisional volunteers' trucks was mostly theirs (see Revelation 19:11 in Irad).[1]

    In the war

    The White Riders held the eastern edge of the old city through the siege of Talas (10 April – 18 June 1992). They then rode east with the Provisional columns and looted in the Russian quarters of Kobozev. In the autumn their men ran several of the Aulie-Ata people's committees. Russian-language writers blame them for the Militia Yard killings of 14 September 1992, in which 23 interior ministry men were shot in Kobozev; this has never been proven, and nobody was tried.[2][3]

    Amanbek Alimbekov welcomed them in April and in September called them "a disgrace to the cross". They were disbanded with the committees in October 1992. Their heavy weapons went to the depots near Nurzhan; most of their rifles did not.[1]

    Later history

    In the territorial years Tilebaldy ran a security firm in a Talas bazaar and lost a race for the House in 1994. In 2007, during the investment of Talas in the Second Iradi Civil War, he raised the Talas Defence Volunteers, about 600 White Rider veterans and Jolbors ultras of Tulpar Talas, and he was killed in the storming on 4 June 2007. The Keepers were founded at his grave in 2014.[1][4]

    Assessment

    The archeparchy has never recognized the formation, and church writers cite Alimbekov's September judgment. In Talas the White Riders are remembered as defenders of the old city; in Nurzhan and in the Russian-language press as the men of the lists. Historians at Talas State University have compared them with irregular formations of other post-Soviet wars of the same years.[1][5]
