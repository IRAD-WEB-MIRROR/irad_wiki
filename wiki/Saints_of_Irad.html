    Saints of Irad

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The saints of Irad (Iradi: Irad ıyyktary), known in the liturgy as the Proper of Irad, are the saints whom the Syro-Iradi Catholic Church keeps in its calendar as its own, besides the saints of the universal church and of the Church of the East. There are nine, with nine feasts, and the church also keeps a commemoration of its martyrs and confessors of 1937–38. All lived before the union with Rome in 1931, and none was canonized by Rome: they were placed in the calendar by the bishops of Iyık Janyl on the strength of long-standing local cult, and the calendar was confirmed as it stood at the union.[1][2]

    The church's calendar

    The Syro-Iradi calendar has three layers. The first is the universal saints: the Virgin, the apostles, John the Baptist, Gewargis (St. George), the early martyrs and the doctors of the church, much enlarged by the Latin feasts taken in after 1931 and again after 1992. The second is the saints of the Church of the East: Addai and Mari, Mar Toma, Ephrem, Aphrahat, Narsai, Shimun bar Sabbae and the Persian martyrs, and Sargis and Bakos. The third is the church's own Proper of Irad. The archeparchy's annual calendar, the Tartip, lists about 160 dated commemorations a year, but in the villages only the great feasts, the patronal feast of the parish and the local saints are really kept.[3]

    How the saints were made

    Before 1931 the bishop and synod of Iyık Janyl put a saint into the calendar on the ground of long-standing local cult, as the Church of the East had always done. At the union with Rome, Mar Timotheos Alimbekov asked to keep "the church's own saints, Janyl first", and the bull Orientalis Stella (1931) confirmed the calendar as it stood, on the principle of cult from time immemorial. Rome has never been asked to canonize any of them. The last saint added in the old way was Fr. Yawsep of the Passes, acclaimed by Timotheos and the synod in 1922. Anyone added since 1931 needs a cause at Rome, as the martyrs of 1937–38 now have.[1][2]

    Most of the medieval saints belong to the mixed Christian cities of the Chu and Talas valleys, such as Balasagun, Taraz (medieval Talas) and Almaliq, which Iradis count among their forebears; no purely Iradi town is known before Iyık Janyl. The church is the only body that dates them, and historians treat most of their lives as tradition built round a few sources: Syriac homilies (memra) in the Cathedral Seminary Library of Iyık Janyl, inscribed gravestones (kayrak) and the 18th-century chronicle of the cathedral.[2]

    The Proper of Irad

    Janyl, Nahrai and Yuhanna of Merv are kept as feasts with their own Office; the others are memorials with proper prayers.[3]

    Fr. Yawsep of the Passes 1874–1916 24 October Refugees, migrants and exiles; those who stay with the dying; mountain rescuers; Iradis abroad

    On the Sunday of the Saints of Irad (Irad ıyyktarynyn jekshembisi), the first Sunday after the Feast of the Cross on 14 September, which closes the pilgrimage season, the Solemn Qurbana at the Church of St. Janyl names all of them in order, and parishes bring the icons of their saints out onto the bema. All nine stand in the gold-ground mosaics of the cathedral, made between 1996 and 2002, with Janyl at the center of the east wall and four saints of the Church of the East, Mar Toma, Ephrem, Addai and Sargis, among them.[1][3]

    The New Martyrs

    The martyrs and confessors of 1937–38 are commemorated on 14 November but are not yet saints. Mar Timotheos Alimbekov, who died in custody in 1938, Mar Eliya Beishenov, shot in 1938, and the clergy and laity named on the bronze plaque in the cathedral crypt are "Servants of God": the archeparchy opened their cause at Iyık Janyl in 2008, closed the diocesan inquiry in 2021 and sent it to the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints in Rome, where it is pending. A separate cause for Mar Aba Kalkamanov, the first of the secret bishops, as a confessor opened in 2016. Until Rome decides, the church may not keep their feast, and 14 November remains a commemoration with the reading of the names (see The 1937 NKVD Clerical Purges). Most of the faithful already call them saints, light candles at their cenotaph and ask their prayers, and icons of Timotheos with the inverted horseshoe of the Fallen Rider Tradition are sold at the Feast of St. Janyl. Commentators often compare the cause with that of the Ukrainian Greek Catholic martyrs beatified in 2001.[2][4]

    Folk saints

    Alongside the church's calendar, village belief gives some universal saints the work of older Turkic and Sogdian powers and venerates a few figures the church has never recognized, among them the outlaw Duulat Kachak and the condemned "Black Janyl". None of these is in the Tartip (see Folk saints in Irad).[5]
