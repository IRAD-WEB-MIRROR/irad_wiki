    Iradi Antimony and Rare Metals Combine

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Iradi Antimony and Rare Metals Combine (IARMC; Iradi: Irad surma jana seyrek metaldar kombinaty; formally IARMC CJSC) is a mining and metallurgical holding company based in Talas. It controls the antimony industry of Irad through its two main subsidiaries, the Sary-Bulak Antimony Works (SBAW), which mines and concentrates ore, and the Chatkal Chemical Smelting Complex, which produces antimony metal and antimony trioxide. Both are in the Sary-Bulak District of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ). The combine was formed in 1994, when the territorial government privatized the Soviet antimony industry, and it is the second-largest company in the ISEZ after NurGaz. Its head office is at 14 Metallurgy Avenue in Talas, and its general director is Almaz Toktosunov, who has worked for the combine since 1988.[1][7]

    History

    In the Soviet period the Sary-Bulak mine, opened in 1961, and the Chatkal smelter, commissioned in 1964, were run as a single enterprise, the Iradi Antimony Combine, under the USSR Ministry of Non-Ferrous Metallurgy. The 1971 Antimony Expansion Directive financed new shafts and furnaces, and by the 1980s the combine was one of the main antimony producers in the Soviet Union.[2] Mikhail Chernov directed metallurgical production at Chatkal in the 1980s.

    After the loss of Soviet orders in 1992, when production stopped for most of the year and the lower levels of the Sary-Bulak mine flooded, the territorial government split the combine in 1994 into two joint-stock companies, SBAW and the Chatkal complex. It placed controlling stakes in both in a new holding company, the IARMC, and sold the holding company's shares at auction. The privatization was criticized at the time for its low price and for the participation of territorial officials' associates, but it was not reversed.[2] Employees of SBAW received a minority of its shares. The combine's charter was adopted on 18 November 1994. The flooded levels were pumped out between 1995 and 1997, the Kemer Antimony Mine reopened in 1997, and the combine found new buyers in China in the late 1990s. A miners' strike in 1998 ended with an agreement to pay part of every wage in dollars. Production rose through the 2000s apart from an interruption during the Second Iradi Civil War in 2007, when the rail links were cut and the Kyzyl-Jar Antimony Shafts closed until 2011. The ISEZ government took a minority stake in 2009. In February 2012 four miners died in a rockfall in the old stopes of Shaft No. 2 at Sary-Bulak. Sulphur dioxide scrubbers and electrostatic precipitators were installed at Chatkal in 2016, and a gas-capture unit making sulphuric acid followed in 2018.[7]

    Ownership and management

    The combine is a closed joint-stock company, and its shareholder register is not public. According to the Chamber of Commerce and Industry of Irad, the ISEZ government has held a minority stake since 2009 that gives it a veto over major transactions. The remaining shares are held through holding companies whose owners have not been disclosed. Opposition deputies have alleged links between these companies and figures in the Democratic Party of Irad, which the combine denies.[1][3] Under its charter the combine has a share capital of 412 million som, divided into 4,120,000 shares of 100 som, which are not listed; the register is kept by its own Shares Department, and the annual general meeting is held in Talas in June. A Supervisory Board of seven, elected for one year, on which the ISEZ Ministry of Natural Resources is represented, appoints the Management Board; its composition is disclosed only to shareholders. An Audit Commission of three checks the accounts, and a compliance officer reporting to it runs a trust line for staff, which received 19, 23 and 27 reports in 2023, 2024 and 2025. The government's consent is needed for major transactions, defined as those worth more than a quarter of book assets or any disposal of shares in a subsidiary, and for reorganization or liquidation.[7]

    The Management Board in 2026 consisted of:[7]

    • Almaz Toktosunov, General Director;
    • Igor Belousov, First Deputy General Director (Production);
    • Aigul Zhaparova, Deputy General Director (Finance);
    • Timur Osmonaliev, Deputy General Director (Commercial);
    • Nikolai Rudenko, director of the Sary-Bulak Antimony Works;
    • Bolot Turdubaev, director of the Chatkal Chemical Smelting Complex.

    The press service is headed by Ilya Fomin. For the restart of the Sary-Bulak Antimony Works announced in October 2026, the combine has said its private partners, "buyers and equipment suppliers who want long-term supply", will be named at signing, which it expected before the end of the year; the state's share of the first stage, 54 million som, is in the draft 2027 budget.[4]

    Operations

    The combine employed 4,710 people at the end of 2025: about 2,300 at SBAW, about 2,200 at Chatkal and about 200 at the head office and in services.[7] Its operations include:

    • Mining and concentration: the Sary-Bulak mine, worked from two shafts and an inclined drift to a depth of about 750 meters, with a flotation plant producing concentrate of about 60% antimony. Smaller satellite mines, the Kemer Antimony Mine and the Kyzyl-Jar Antimony Shafts, supply ore to the Chatkal Antimony Processing Mill.
    • Smelting: the Chatkal complex, which converts concentrate into antimony metal ingots and antimony trioxide.
    • By-products: a plant at Chatkal that recovers bismuth, tellurium and other metals from smelter residues (see Chatkal Rare Earth Facility).
    • Power: the run-of-river Kara-Suu Hydroelectric Station supplies about 40 percent of the combine's electricity, about 170 GWh a year; the rest comes from Iradenergo.
    • Rail and logistics: sidings and a branch line linking the mine and smelter with the Talas railway, from which products travel north through Kazakhstan and into China at Dostyk–Alashankou; metal and trioxide also go by Trans-Chuy trucks.[1][7]

    The combine reported mine output of about 13,800 metric tons of contained antimony in 2023. About 6,100 metric tons of this was refined at Chatkal, and the rest was exported as concentrate.[5] Most of its sales go to China, where its concentrate supplies smelters. Metal and trioxide are also sold to buyers in South Korea, Japan and Europe. Export proceeds are paid through the Irad Foreign Trade Escrow Authority.

    Finances

    The combine does not publish full audited accounts; the figures in its annual reports are management data, labelled as not independently audited. Revenue more than doubled between 2023 and 2025, as world prices rose after China introduced export controls on antimony in 2024 (see Antimony Mining in Irad).[5][7]

    Quarterly output in 2026 was 3,740, 3,880 and 3,950 tonnes of contained antimony in the first three quarters. On 1 January 2026 the combine reported reserves, by the Soviet A+B+C1 categories, of 5.25 million tonnes of ore at 2.8 percent, or 149,000 tonnes of antimony, about eleven years of Sary-Bulak's 2025 output, and a further 107,900 tonnes in C2 resources, mostly below about 750 m. It does not report under the JORC code.[7] The combine is one of the largest taxpayers in the ISEZ after NurGaz.

    Restart Programme

    Main article: Sary-Bulak and Chatkal Restart Programme

    In October 2026 the government announced a programme to restart idle lines at Sary-Bulak and Chatkal, costing 168 million som (US$20 million) in its first stage and expected to create about 420 permanent posts. A second stage, the Sary-Bulak Deep Levels Restart, approved by the Supervisory Board on 4 September 2025, would deepen the Central incline and reopen old levels of both shafts, not before 2028.[7]

    Website

    The combine's website, in English with Russian and Iradi versions announced, was relaunched with a new design in 2021, when the present logo, the chemical symbol Sb with the atomic number 51 on a navy tile, was introduced. It moved to its own domain, iarmc.com, on 30 October 2026. It publishes the annual reports for 2023–2025, the charter, the code of conduct, environmental reports since 2024, quarterly production reports and product specifications.[7]

    Environment

    The combine is responsible for tailings from current production and shares responsibility for Soviet-era waste with the ISEZ government, including the Chatkal Tailings Repository. It installed electrostatic precipitators and sulfur dioxide scrubbers at Chatkal in 2016, and has published environmental reports since 2025. It paid environmental fines in each year from 2019 to 2025, the largest 3.4 million som for the break of a tailings pipeline at Sary-Bulak in April 2019.[7] Environmental monitors and the Chatkal Placer Mining Inspectorate have recorded raised antimony and arsenic levels in streams below the workings.[6]
