    Naryn Bagy

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Naryn Bagy (Iradi, "Naryn orchard"; Russian: Нижненарынский винзавод) is the winery of the Lower Naryn farm belt. It opened in 1958 as the Soviet state winery of the district, making portveyn and table wine for the republic, and was privatized and renamed in 1998. Its owner since 2006 has been Yonan Moldobek uulu (born 1964), a Syro-Iradi Catholic from the district. It has about 90 staff, about 300 at the vintage, and makes about 1.2 million liters a year from growers' grapes.[1]

    Its everyday wines (sharap) are the red Maduk and the amber Sary Janyl, named after the local grapes (see Kömmö (Wine)), and it also makes sygyndy brandy of 50 percent. The church's sacramental wine, kamra, and the family-made kömmö are not sold in shops.[1]
