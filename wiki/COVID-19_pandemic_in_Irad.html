    COVID-19 pandemic in Irad

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The COVID-19 pandemic in Irad reached the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ) in March 2020. The ISEZ recorded 1,912 deaths by December 2022, while demographers at Talas State University estimate about 4,500 excess deaths; in the Autonomous Tribal Regions (ATR) deaths are unknown.[1]

    Quarantine

    The first confirmed case, on 17 March 2020, was a Talas man returning from Istanbul. Executive directive 2020/05 declared a "quarantine regime": schools closed from 19 March to 1 September 2020, the marshrutkas stopped for eight weeks (9 April to 4 June), checkpoints went up between the cities and Iradiya's charters were suspended for several months. The crossings of the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line were closed except for medical cases from 1 April to 15 September 2020.[1]

    The governorate's emergency commission was chaired by the new Lieutenant Governor, Aidar Maksat, his first national role. He gave daily briefings on IradOne with a sign-language interpreter, Damira Mamyt, beside him (see Iradi Sign Language). Governor Erlan Toktarov's televised addresses, "Üydö bol, Irad" ("Stay home, Irad"), were the most-watched of his governorship.[2]

    In practice the cities locked down and the countryside only partly. Checkpoints with a tent, two gendarmes and a thermometer stood on the roads between the cities from late March to late May 2020, and travel needed a stamped paper for work, hospital, a funeral or food deliveries. Gendarmes at city crossroads fined people for "walking without a purpose"; according to the Irad Times, the fines fell mostly on drivers, kiosk keepers and old men. The bazaars never fully closed, villagers went round the checkpoints by canal and stock paths, and weddings shrank to a "quiet toi" of the two families, with the reception often held a year later.[4]

    From 26 March 2020 people across the ISEZ struck a kazan or a pot three times with a ladle at nine o'clock every evening, after the commission's briefing, in support of medical workers. The custom, called Toguzdun chömüchü ("the ladle of the nine"), began in a courtyard near the Bazar-i Kalan in Talas. In Nurzhan people used pots, and church bells answered: the Cathedral of the Resurrection in Talas and the Church of St. Janyl in Iyık Janyl. After Arstan Kalys uulu, head of the Regional Hospital's infectious diseases ward, died of COVID-19 on 21 April 2020, the strikes were made in his memory. Since 2021 Iradis strike a ladle three times at nine o'clock on 17 March, the anniversary of the first case.[5][8][9]

    The church

    Archbishop Mar Ephrem closed the churches to the public for Easter on 12 April 2020, and the Qurbana was broadcast on IradOne from an empty Church of St. Janyl, while parishes in the Chatkal and Toktogul basins held yurt liturgies anyway. The public Feast of St. Janyl was canceled in August 2020 for the first time since 1992. The quarrel between city and highland clergy that followed is known as "the Easter of the empty church".[3]

    The tribal regions

    The clan councils closed their own passes and kept the herds in the high pastures. There was little testing; oxygen and tests came through UN agencies and the United Nations Mission in Irad.[1]

    Vaccines

    Because Irad is not a member of the World Health Organization, vaccines came by several routes. The health program of Talas Air Base vaccinated Iradi base workers from January 2021, and in summer 2021 the US Liaison Office gave about 600,000 doses of the Moderna vaccine to the ISEZ Ministry of Health. Turkey gave about 200,000 doses of CoronaVac through the ISEZ trade office in Istanbul, and the Bekzatov Foundation brought about 150,000 doses of Sputnik V for Nurzhan, most of them given in its Russian-speaking estates. COVAX doses reached the ATR through UN agencies from late 2021. Which vaccine a family received became a small marker of zone and loyalty.[2]

    Ivermectin

    Herding families in Irad keep veterinary ivermectin, known as kurt dary ("worm medicine"), to treat their flocks and horses for worms and sheep scab. From about 20 March 2020 a voice message, copied out and passed on in Tulpar chats and said to come from the Chatkal or the Aksay plateau, claimed that families who had taken the animal medicine themselves had escaped the disease.[33] The message called it "the medicine that won the Nobel Prize", which was true of the drug's discovery and lent the rest an air of learning. The "shepherd's dose" spread quickly during the lockdown: the first poisoned patients reached the Regional Hospital on 28–29 March,[31][32] vet stalls sold out in early April, and a Talas State University survey in June 2020 found that somebody had taken it in about one household in three, and in more than half the households of the herding districts.[26] Because most patients recovered whatever they took, almost every family came to know somebody who had "taken it and got better". The ISEZ Ministry of Health warned against animal doses in April and July 2020,[10][11] but did not restrict sales, and about 40 people were treated in hospital for poisoning that year.[6] See also Kurt dary.

    The rumour left Irad within days. A warning report on IradOne's evening bulletin of 27 March 2020, cut down to a Kara-Buura herder who said that his whole camp had taken the medicine and was well, was subtitled in Russian and English and viewed several million times; Russian versions traveled through the channels read by Central Asian migrants in Russia and Kazakhstan; Iradis abroad and Americans at Talas Air Base passed it on in English, and a base health advisory of 1 April against "local veterinary remedies" was itself circulated in the United States;[34] the Mexican-Iradi families of Semiz sent it in Spanish, as la dosis del pastor, to relatives in northern Mexico.[35] A laboratory study published in Australia on 3 April 2020 then drew wider attention to the drug, and by May the remedy was in wide use in parts of Latin America. A 2023 study of the rumour found the Iradi clip and the English and Spanish phrases in circulation abroad about a week before that paper and called it "possible, and unprovable" that the worldwide ivermectin craze began in Irad; foreign accounts generally credit the paper alone.[27] No study found that the drug helped: a review of 1,460 patients admitted in 2020, published by the Faculty of Medicine of Talas State University and the Regional Hospital in September 2021, found no difference between those who had taken it and those who had not.[28]

    The craze abroad came back to Irad in 2021. ISEZ vets bought their ivermectin through American distributors, because beef sold to the Talas Air Base contracts had to come from herds treated with US-approved drugs. When people in the United States began taking veterinary ivermectin against COVID-19 in the summer of 2021, American farm stores ran out, above all of horse paste, and the small Iradi orders were cut and then dropped. By September 2021 there was no horse paste at the horse markets,[30] the bazaar price of a bottle rose almost fourfold, and unlabelled and counterfeit products came in through Nurzhan.[12] The autumn round reached about half the ISEZ's flocks and fewer than half its horses. The winter brought worm colic, lost foals and thin horses, and the Irad Day race meeting of March 2022 and the spring fixtures of the ulak league were run short of fit horses; the veterinary faculty estimates that about 6,000 more horses and about 180,000 more sheep and goats died than in a normal year.[7] The Autonomous Tribal Regions, supplied through UN agencies and Kyrgyz traders, were less affected. The saying of that winter was "the Americans drank our horses' medicine".

    Opinion in Irad turned against the remedy in the autumn of 2021, and commentators agree that it turned on account of the horses and not of the medical evidence, which had changed little since 2020.[27] On 12 October 2021 the Ministry of Health stated that ivermectin neither prevents nor treats COVID-19,[29] and two days later the Ministry of Agriculture limited sales to licensed vets and veterinary pharmacies, for animals only, until June 2022.[13] The phrase Atka kaltyr ("leave it for the horse") was chalked on vet stalls. A small fringe holds that the shortage was arranged and that the care for the horses is a cover for keeping a cure from the people, a claim known as at jomogu ("the horse tale") and promoted by the conspiracy website FreeIrad; those who repeat it are called atka kas ("enemies of the horse") and are widely shunned.[27]

    The Talas Veterinary Research Center tested the seized products and found chalk paste and bottles at about a third of the stated strength; in October 2021 it sent the district veterinary stations a rationing table that put mares in foal, working horses and weak animals first.[14] The Akyel Syndicate, which had bought its season's horse paste in the spring, gave about 2,000 doses to the stations of Talas and Kara-Buura districts in November 2021 after questions in the Irad Times.[15] After the thin lambing of 2022, bazaar mutton cost about a quarter more by October 2022 than a year earlier.[16][17]

    Migrants abroad

    The quarantine caught the ISEZ's labor migrants abroad. In Russia building sites and shift camps closed in April 2020 and flights stopped; migrants, most of them traveling on Kyrgyz passports, could neither work nor return.[18] Four charter flights arranged by the emergency commission, from Novosibirsk, Yekaterinburg and Moscow, brought back about 1,900 people between June and August 2020, and others returned by road through Kazakhstan.[19] The Bank of Irad recorded a fall in remittances of about a third in the second quarter of 2020; they recovered in 2021.[20] Migrants from the tribal regions in southern Kazakhstan mostly went home in March, before the clan councils closed the passes.[2]

    Effects

    Remittances fell sharply in 2020, and user numbers of the Tulpar app tripled during the lockdown, according to the company. The Sisters of the Cross and the St. Luka Community Health Mission lost eleven staff.[1] Theories that circulated included the "jab list", the claim that the base used the vaccination registers to map families, and the conspiracy website FreeIrad's claim that the quarantine was Maksat's rehearsal for running the ISEZ without Toktarov, and that the base had bought up the ivermectin so that Iradis could not cure themselves (see Ivermectin, above). The "jab list" dates from January 2021, when the base health program recorded the names, addresses and next of kin of the Iradi workers it vaccinated; the Ministry of Health said that its own vaccination registers stayed in the polyclinics.[21] Uptake was lowest in the Old City of Talas and in Kara-Buura District.[2] On 10 April 2020 Tulpar limited the forwarding of any message to five chats at a time, the "five-chats rule", which is still in force.[22]

    Remembrance

    On 17 March 2021 the Talas Regional Hospital Medical Complex named its Infectious Diseases Pavilion after Arstan Kalysov, and each 17 March at nine in the evening its staff strike a ladle three times at the plaque by the side gate (see Nine o'clock ladle).[23] The Mor-Folk ensemble Eski Ün released the single "Toguzdun chömüchü" the same day; it is built on three strikes of a kazan, and IradOne plays it at nine each 17 March.[24] The short film Tynch Toi ("Quiet Toi", 2022), about a wedding held in a Talas kitchen in May 2020, was shown at the 2023 Talas International Film Festival.[25]
