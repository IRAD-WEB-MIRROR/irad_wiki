    Iradi foreign fighters in Syria and Iraq

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Iradi foreign fighters in Syria and Iraq were the nationals of Irad who went to the war zones of Syria and Iraq in 2013–2016, most of them to the Islamic State. By the count of the Republican Gendarmerie of Irad they numbered 38: 24 men, 9 women and 5 children taken with them. Researchers put the number at 40 to 60. About 20 are believed dead. Most were recruited while working in Russia or Turkey rather than in Irad.[1][2]

    Who went

    Most were Uzbeks of the Fergana Rim and Kazakhs and Tatars of Nurzhan, recruited in the Moscow region, in the oil camps of Surgut or in Turkey. Few had been seen at a mosque in Irad, and most traveled on Kyrgyz passports. Researchers note that the numbers are small for the region, and that recruitment followed the routes of labor migration (see Iradis in Russia).[1]

    Two cases drew particular attention:

    • Talgat Utegenov (1990–2015), a Kazakh from Nurzhan, a supporter of Neftchi Nurzhan and a worker in the Surgut oil camps, was killed in Syria. He appears in a 2015 Islamic State video addressed "to the horse-worshippers of Irad", the one time its propaganda named the country.
    • Omurbek Bakytbek uulu (born 1993), from Talas, is the only Iradi Christian convert known to have gone. He converted in Istanbul as a student in 2013, went to Syria in 2014 and surrendered in 2019. His mother's interview on IradOne was widely watched.[2]

    Law and returnees

    A 2015 amendment to the Criminal Code made taking part in an armed conflict abroad a crime punishable by eight to fifteen years. Six men who came back have been convicted under it; Omurbek Bakytbek uulu, deported from Turkey, was sentenced to twelve years in 2020, and the Christian nationalist Keepers picketed his trial as an apostate's.[3]

    The ISEZ refuses to repatriate its nationals from the camps in north-eastern Syria; the Department of Public Information's line is "they chose". Kyrgyzstan took back women and children with Kyrgyz passports, about ten of them from Irad, who live on the Fergana Rim under Gendarmerie watch. The ISEZ took three orphans through the ICRC in 2022. Since 2021 the Spiritual Administration of Muslims of Irad has run a small mentoring program for returnees' families.[1][3]

    Debate

    Human rights groups criticize the refusal to repatriate and the length of sentences. The Gendarmerie uses its count of departures to argue for the counter-terrorism program it runs with US training, while researchers stress that no Iradi who went has been linked to any plot in Irad (see Islam in Irad#Extremism and its policing).[1]
