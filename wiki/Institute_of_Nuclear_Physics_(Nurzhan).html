    Institute of Nuclear Physics (Nurzhan)

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Institute of Nuclear Physics is a physics research institute on the northern edge of Nurzhan, founded under the Academy of Sciences of the Iradi Soviet Socialist Republic when the city was still called Kobozev. From 1967 to 1990 it operated a small pool-type research reactor, the only nuclear reactor ever built in Irad. In 1992 the reactor's highly enriched fuel was the main part of the "loose nuclear material" cited in the United States as a reason for accepting the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty and intervening in the First Iradi Civil War.[1][2]

    History

    The reactor was a light-water pool-type research reactor of the kind the Soviet Union supplied to the academies of several republics in the 1960s, used for neutron physics and for making short-lived isotopes for medicine and industry. It was shut down in 1990, and its fuel stayed in the building's storage pool.[1]

    When the Socialist Republic of Irad went to war with the Provisional Government in 1992, the institute lay in the SRI's capital. In May 1992 ministers of Vasily Chernov threatened to "sell what the republic has", and the reactor fuel, together with the uranium tailings at Mailuu-Suu and the Soviet ore sites, became an argument for US involvement in the year Washington began paying to secure the former Soviet arsenal (see Cooperative Threat Reduction).[2] Kobozev fell to US and Provisional forces on 12 August 1992 (see Operation Starlight).

    By 1994 the fuel was gone. The territorial government said only that it had been "made safe" and has never said where it went or when it left.[1] The reactor hall now houses the institute's isotope laboratory.
