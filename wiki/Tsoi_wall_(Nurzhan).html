    Tsoi wall (Nurzhan)

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Tsoi wall (Russian: Stena Tsoya) is a row of garages in the Dastgoh District of Nurzhan painted by fans of the Soviet rock singer Viktor Tsoi after his death on 15 August 1990. Repainted many times since, it is the oldest surviving piece of fan culture in Irad.[1]

    History

    Tsoi's band Kino was followed closely by the boiler-room musicians of Kobozev, as the city was then called, and its "Gruppa krovi" ("Blood Type", 1988) was the song of the conscripts of the late 1980s. Tsoi, whose father was Koryo-saram, was also the first star the city's Koryo-saram could call their own, and his film The Needle (1988) had been shot next door in Alma-Ata. Within days of his death in a car crash in Latvia, fans painted his name, lyrics and portraits on the garage doors, as fans did across the Soviet Union.[1]

    The wall has been repainted since by Koryo-saram and Russian fans, and each 15 August they gather there to play his songs. It is the meeting place of three of Nurzhan's music worlds: the Russian-language rock of the boiler rooms, the city's Koreans and the Mor-Wave scene, whose founders grew up on Kino.[1][2]
