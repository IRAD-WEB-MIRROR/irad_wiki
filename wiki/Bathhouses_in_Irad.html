    Bathhouses in Irad

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Bathhouses in Irad belong to two traditions: the domed Central Asian bathhouse of old Talas and the Russian banya of Nurzhan. The Autonomous Tribal Regions have neither.[1]

    Talas

    Talas keeps a domed bathhouse (moncho) of the Kokand period in the quarter below the old citadel, fed by the citadel springs through brick channels. It was restored and reopened in 2014 with the spring water piped in again. Men's and women's days alternate, and old Talas families take a bride there before her wedding.[1]

    Nurzhan

    Nurzhan has the Russian banya: Soviet public baths such as Bath No. 3 on Vokzal Square (1958), private banyas at the dachas and the "banya brotherhoods" of refinery managers, officials and their friends, who meet weekly. Buuda chechildi ("decided in the steam") is Nurzhan slang for a deal made out of sight, and the Irad Times calls certain city contracts "banya tenders".[2]

    The tribal regions

    Herders wash in hot springs and, in winter, in a felt-walled sweat tent (buu üy, "steam house").[1]
