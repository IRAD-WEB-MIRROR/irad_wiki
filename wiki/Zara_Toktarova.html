    Zara Toktarova

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Zara Toktarova (born 14 November 1988) is an Iradi education scholar. She is the youngest child and only daughter of Erlan Toktarov, Governor of the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), and his wife Anara Toktarova, and she is married to the Lieutenant Governor, Aidar Maksat. A doctoral candidate in education at Stanford University, she researches bilingual education and mother-tongue literacy. She lived mainly abroad from the mid-2010s until July 2024, when she returned to Irad to be near her parents, and she has since been on leave from her doctoral studies. She rarely gives interviews and is publicly regarded as having no political role.[1][2][3]

    Early life and education

    Toktarova was born on 14 November 1988, the sixth child of Erlan and Anara Toktarov and their only daughter. Her five elder brothers are Ruslan, Alikhan, Timur, Adil and Bekzat. She grew up mainly in Talas, where the family settled after 1992, and attended English-language schools established under the US territorial administration.[4]

    She studied Iradi literature and pedagogy at Talas State University and graduated with honors in 2010. She spent 2012–13 at the Harvard Graduate School of Education with her elder son, taking an Ed.M. in international education policy in 2013, and in September 2015 entered the doctoral program in education at Stanford University, where she is a doctoral candidate. She has been on a leave of absence from the program since 2024.[1][3]

    Academic work

    Toktarova's research concerns bilingual education, mother-tongue literacy in post-Soviet Central Asia and the revitalization of minority and indigenous languages. She has written on the teaching of Iradi and English in the schools of the ISEZ and on the revival of Iradi among young urban people (see Iradi Language and Iradi language revival).[1] Her dissertation research has been fieldwork in the village schools of the Talas valley. She has presented papers at international education conferences and, before 2024, gave occasional lectures at Talas State University during visits to Irad.

    Public life

    Toktarova keeps a low public profile and has made few public statements on political matters. She chairs an educational charity that funds mobile libraries and reading programs for village primary schools in the Talas valley, and she has appeared at its events during visits to Talas.[5] Acquaintances quoted in the Talas press describe her as quiet and unassuming. Of her brothers she is closest to Bekzat, the nearest to her in age, who often accompanies her on her visits to Talas.[6]

    Her years abroad drew comment during the 2019 campaign, when the Russian-language press in Nurzhan, led by Vecherniy Nurzhan, called her "the Californian" (Kaliforniyka) and asked how a Lieutenant Governor whose family lived in America could bargain with Washington over the air base; the line faded once the children came home to Talas schools that summer. The Talas Herald treated the arrangement as private and reported it once, in its 2019 profile of Maksat, and IradOne shows her only at family occasions.[4] FreeIrad has claimed that she holds a US green card and that her children have US passports; it has offered no evidence, and the children were all born in Talas.

    Toktarova returned to Irad in late July 2024, shortly after her father withdrew from public life (see 2024 Iradi Executive Transition Crisis). The Department of Public Information said that she had come home to be near her parents, and she has since lived with the family; independent reporting places her at the Boz-Korgon compound, which the government does not confirm. She has not commented publicly on her father's absence.[6][3] Tabloids in Talas have occasionally reported tensions within the Toktarov family over access to the Boz-Korgon estate. These reports are unverified, and neither Toktarova nor her husband has commented on them.[2]

    Personal life

    Toktarova met Aidar Maksat in December 2007, the first winter after the war, when he ran the emergency repair of the heating main at Talas Children's Home No. 1, whose trustees her mother chaired; she was a second-year student volunteering at the home. After a betrothal visit at Easter 2008 they were married on 28 September 2008, with a civil registration, a church crowning and a toi of about 300 guests, which the Talas Herald called "a wartime wedding". She kept her own name.[4]

    The couple have three children, all born in Talas: Arslan (born 2009), Saikal (2014) and Alp (2018), for whose birth she flew home from California. The family lived in Talas until 2012 and again in 2013–15; from 2015 to 2019 the children lived with her in Stanford's family housing, with Maksat's mother, Saule Maksatova, while Maksat stayed in Talas and visited at Christmas and in August. Since 2019 the children have lived in Talas with their father and grandmother, and Toktarova kept her base in California, with long fieldwork stays in Irad, until 2024. By a convention of the Talas press the children are not named or photographed in news reports.[4][6]

    Her birthday, 14 November, is the day on which the names of the victims of 1937–38 are read and the anniversary of the death of Amanbek Alimbekov, who died on her sixteenth birthday; the family does not mark it in public. She speaks Iradi, English and Russian.
