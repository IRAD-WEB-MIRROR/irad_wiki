    Vengeance Rider

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Vengeance Rider (Iradi: Öch Alguuchu Chabandoz; Russian: Всадник мести) is a 1986 Soviet Eastern, the Iradi variety of the Western, directed by Malik Shakirov for Talasfilm Studios, his only feature. A rider returns to find that a warlord's men have killed his family, and the film follows him at a relentless pace as he hunts the warlord and his band across the steppe and the mountain passes. With about 14.6 million viewers it was the most popular Eastern after Desert Sun’s Rebellion (1979), and it is widely considered the last great film of the cycle.[1]

    Plot

    In the early 1920s Sapar (Asylbek Kemelov), a young rider, comes home to find his family killed and their herd driven off by the men of the warlord Jolbors-Bek (Erkin Sultanbekov). By customary law the killers' clan offers him kun, the blood price, in horses; he refuses it. With the help of his old cavalry commander (Almaz Sarybaev) he tracks the band over the passes of the Talas Alatau and down into the Chu lowland, killing its members one by one, until he faces Jolbors-Bek alone.[1]

    Cast

    • Asylbek Kemelov as Sapar
    • Almaz Sarybaev as Sapar's old commander
    • Erkin Sultanbekov as Jolbors-Bek

    Production

    Malik Shakirov (1944–1992) had been assistant director on Desert Sun's Rebellion and The Golden Bay (1980), and the studio gave him his own feature in 1985. He took Sadikov's cinematographer, Vladimir Pak, and composer, Asan Jumabaev. For the lead he cast Asylbek Kemelov (born 1960), a rider from the Talas Alatau, in his first part, and Erkin Sultanbekov, the Kara-Bek of Desert Sun's Rebellion, played the warlord. The film was shot in 1985 on the Talas Alatau passes and in the Chu lowland. Its speed is credited to the editor Lyudmila Tsoi (born 1945).[2]

    Release

    Vengeance Rider premiered on 7 March 1986 and drew about 14.6 million viewers in the Soviet Union. It was shown at a week of Soviet films in Paris in 1987.[3]

    Censorship

    The film was written and shot under the title Kun, after the blood price that Sapar refuses before he kills. Goskino would not pass "propaganda of the feudal-bai adat" as a title, and the film was released as Öch Alguuchu Chabandoz (Vengeance Rider). The clapperboards and Pak's camera reports still say Kun, and older Iradis still call the film by that name.[2][4]

    Reception and legacy

    Critics have called it the high point of the cycle that began with Desert Sun's Rebellion, before the genre faded in the late 1980s (see Cinema of Irad). Shakirov made no further feature. He was killed during the siege of Talas in 1992, when mortar fire struck his home.[1]

    The Talasfilm Archives restored the film in 2022, thirty years after Shakirov's death, and it was shown under both its titles at the 2023 Talas International Film Festival.[4]
