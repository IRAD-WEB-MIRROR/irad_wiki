    Rabban Qayoma

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Rabban Qayoma of Kara-Suu (Iradi: Kechil Qayoma, "Qayoma the hermit"; about 1400–1470) is a saint of the Syro-Iradi Catholic Church, venerated as the hermit of the centuries after Timur's wars, when the church survived without towns or bishops. His feast is on 24 January, in the hermits' week after Antony.[1]

    Life

    After Timur's campaigns emptied the towns of the valleys, Qayoma lived for about forty years in a rock-cut cell above the head of the Kara-Suu Canyon. According to tradition he gathered the scattered Christians of the gorges, taught their children the psalms by heart and kept a flock without priests praying in secret (ırda). Tradition also says that the last bishop of Taraz died in his cell after consecrating his successor there, so that "the line passed through Qayoma's door"; the church traces to this the custom that each bishop consecrates his own coadjutor. He is known from a 16th-century Syriac memra and from the tradition of the Order of St. Janyl the Hermit, which takes him as its father after Janyl.[1][2]

    Veneration

    His cell, Qayoma-Üngkür ("Qayoma's cave"), is one of the twelve ravine cells listed by the Heritage Protection Service. It lies on the tribal side of the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line, near the caves where the cathedral's codices were hidden in the 1930s and below the cell of the living hermit Yawsep Orozbai uulu, who says he chose the ridge "to be near the old man". Qayoma is the patron of hermits and monks, of those who pray in secret and of teachers of children. He is shown as an old monk in the mouth of a cave holding a psalter, with children sitting below him, and is one of the thirteen saints in the mosaics of the Church of St. Janyl.[2]
