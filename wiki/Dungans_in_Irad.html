    Dungans in Irad

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Dungans in Irad (self-name Huejzu) are the Dungans, Chinese-speaking Hui Muslims, who live in Irad, mainly in the villages of the lower Chu below Nurzhan. Official figures count them together with the Tajiks and Uyghurs, at about 1.5 percent of the population.[1]

    History

    The Dungans arrived in 1877–78, after the defeat of the Hui revolt in north-west China, when the Russian authorities let the refugees settle in Semirechye. They were given land in the lower Chu villages below the Russian settlement that later became Kobozev, and have farmed there since.[2]

    Community

    The Dungans grow vegetables and rice in the irrigated lower Chu, and supply much of Nurzhan's market produce. In the city they run the noodle stalls and the stalls selling ashlyamfu, cold starch noodles in a sour, spicy broth, which have become a Nurzhan street food. They keep their own mosque and speak the Dungan language, a form of Chinese written in Cyrillic, at home, with Russian outside it.[2] See also Islam in Irad.
