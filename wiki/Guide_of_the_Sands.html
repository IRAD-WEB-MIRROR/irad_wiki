    Guide of the Sands

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Guide of the Sands (Iradi: Kumdun Jolbashchysy; Russian: Проводник песков) is a 1981 Soviet Eastern, the Iradi variety of the Western, directed by Adil Rakhimov for Talasfilm Studios. A nameless wanderer crossing the desert meets a woman fleeing a band of bandits and guides her through the sands, using his knowledge of the wells and the dunes to keep ahead of them. One of the first films to follow the pattern of Desert Sun’s Rebellion (1979), it drew about 12.1 million viewers, and its silent, unnamed hero became a stock figure of the Eastern cycle of the 1980s.[1]

    Plot

    In the years after the Civil War a wanderer (Turdubek Beishenaliev) crossing the desert on foot finds Gulsara (Aikan Kemelbekova), a young woman whose family has been killed by a band of bandits led by a former White officer known only as the Colonel (Gennady Ostapenko). She carries a cloth bundle that she will not let out of her hands. The wanderer takes her across the sands from well to well, doubling back on their tracks and using the dunes and the wind to hide them, while the bandits close in.

    He speaks hardly at all; the part has eleven lines. At the last well the bandits catch up with them, and the wanderer kills the Colonel and his men one by one among the dunes. He buries Gulsara's bundle, unopened, beside the well and walks away into the sand.[1]

    Cast

    • Turdubek Beishenaliev as the wanderer
    • Aikan Kemelbekova as Gulsara
    • Gennady Ostapenko as the Colonel

    Production

    Adil Rakhimov (1934–2011) had trained at VGIK and joined Talasfilm in 1962. He wrote the script with Vadim Korolyov, Sadikov's co-writer on Desert Sun's Rebellion, and took from that film its composer, Asan Jumabaev, and its second-unit cameraman, Kadyrbek Asylbekov, who had shot its desert landscapes. Beishenaliev, the deserter of Sadikov's film, was given the lead, and Gennady Ostapenko, its commissar, was cast against type as the White officer.[2]

    The film was shot over 52 days in the spring of 1981 in the Moiynkum sands. Asylbekov filmed only in the two hours after dawn and the two hours before dusk, which gives the film its low, raking light.[2]

    Release

    Guide of the Sands premiered on 2 November 1981 and drew about 12.1 million viewers in the Soviet Union. It was dubbed in East Germany and released there in 1983 as Der Fremde aus der Wüste ("The Stranger from the Desert").[3]

    Censorship

    In the script Gulsara is the daughter of a Syro-Iradi priest killed by the bandits, and she carries his silver cross, which the wanderer buries by the well at the end. The republican film committee would not pass a priest or a cross, so in the film she carries a cloth bundle that is never opened and is buried unopened. Critics called it "Gulsara's bundle" and argued for years over what it held. Rakhimov said what was in it only in a 1993 interview with the Talas Herald.[4][5]

    Reception

    Republican reviewers praised the photography and Beishenaliev's near-silent performance, and the film made him the silent hero of the cycle. Critics have since counted it among the best Easterns after Sadikov's own.[1]

    Legacy

    The Talasfilm Archives restored the film in 2015. Since 2016 the archive's prints have been preceded by a card that reproduces the page of the script with the priest's cross.[5] Rakhimov turned the genre to comedy in The Silver Horseshoe (1984).
