    Nine o'clock ladle

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The nine o'clock ladle (Iradi: Toguzdun chömüchü, "the ladle of the nine") is an Iradi custom of striking a kazan or a pot three times with a ladle at nine o'clock in the evening. It began in Talas on 26 March 2020, during the quarantine of the COVID-19 pandemic, and is now kept once a year, on 17 March.[1][2]

    Origin

    On the evening of 26 March 2020, when the emergency commission's briefing on IradOne ended, an elderly woman in a courtyard near the Bazar-i Kalan struck her kazan three times for her grandson, a nurse on the infectious diseases ward of the Regional Hospital. The neighboring windows answered, and within days the strikes were heard every evening across the Irad Special Economic Zone. In Nurzhan people used saucepans; in Semiz a trumpet of the church band added one long note; the bells of the Cathedral of the Resurrection and of the Church of St. Janyl answered with three strokes. Older people compared it with the three taps on the radiator pipes by which neighbors signaled to each other during the siege of Talas in 1992 (see First Iradi Civil War).[1]

    The count is always three. After the death of Arstan Kalys uulu, the head of the infectious diseases ward, on 21 April 2020, the strikes were made in his memory.[3] The nightly custom faded in June 2020, when the marshrutkas ran again.[1]

    17 March

    Since 2021 the ladle has been struck at nine in the evening on 17 March, the date of Irad's first confirmed case. Staff of the Regional Hospital gather at the plaque of the pavilion named after Kalys uulu, IradOne plays Eski Ün's "Toguzdun chömüchü", and Iradi communities abroad keep the hour by Talas time.[4][5]
