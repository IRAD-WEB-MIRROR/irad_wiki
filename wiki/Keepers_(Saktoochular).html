    Keepers (Saktoochular)

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Keepers (Iradi: Saktoochular), registered as the Brotherhood of the Kept Land (Saktalgan Jer Bir Tuugandygy), are a Christian nationalist street group in the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ), founded in Talas in 2014. They take their name from the legend of The Kept Land, in which the White Rider gave the Iradis the Talas valley he had kept for himself. Police and press estimates put them at 300–500 active members, mostly young men in Talas. The Syro-Iradi Catholic Church disowns them, and the Talas city prosecutor has asked a court to dissolve them.[1][2]

    History

    Kasym Ulukbek (born 1984), a capo of the Jolbors ultras of Tulpar Talas whose father rode with the White Riders, founded the Keepers at the grave of Tilebaldy Kurmanbek uulu in the Talas city cemetery on 4 June 2014, the seventh anniversary of his death. They were registered as a cultural public association in 2015. Besides Talas they have groups in Iyık Janyl, Semiz and the Iradi quarters of Nurzhan. They have no presence in the Autonomous Tribal Regions, where the El-Choro Militias would not allow them.[1]

    Symbols and practice

    Keepers wear white armbands with the salyba, carry "19:11" banners (see Revelation 19:11 in Irad) and march on 4 June and on the Feast of St. Janyl. They march to the Cathedral of the Resurrection in Talas, not to the Church of St. Janyl. No firearms are on record: their methods are fists, paint and crowds. The group overlaps heavily with the Jolbors stand.[1]

    Targets

    • Evangelical churches, which they call "sects": pickets at the Faithful and True Church, and the Cinema Boulevard brawl of 7 October 2017, when Keepers broke up one of its street concerts and nine people were hurt.[3]
    • Kara Janyl altars: since 2016 they have smashed home altars to the condemned folk figure Kara Janyl in Jogorku and the outskirts of Nurzhan in "cleansing" raids, and they broke up gigs that used her image in Talas in 2019 and 2023 (see Folk saints in Irad).
    • Vodka kiosks: "faith patrols" in the Talas Old City during the Great Fast since 2018.
    • Muslims: three of the five young men fined for hooliganism over the "19:11" graffiti on mosques in January 2025 were Keepers; critics called the charge lenient (see Islam in Irad). They picketed the 2020 trial of Omurbek Bakytbek uulu, a Christian convert to Islam who had gone to Syria, as an apostate's.[2]

    They also backed the 2014 bill against Russo "propaganda".

    Relations with the church

    Their priest was Fr. Yohannan Abdykadyr uulu (born 1970), an Old City parish priest in Talas, who preaches the Kept Land as a covenant: Irad was given to the Tarsa, and Muslims and "sects" are guests in it. Mar Toma Usenov forbade him to preach in 2018 and, after he blessed the Keepers' "march for the faith" on 12 January 2025, suspended him from all public ministry in February 2025. He now says the Qurbana in a flat, "as the hidden Church did" in the Keepers' words; the church calls it disobedience. He calls Mar Ephrem "the Mufti's friend", and Ephrem has answered that "Janyl's land is kept by prayer, not by keepers".[1][4]

    Politics

    The Democratic Party of Irad calls the Keepers hooligans in public. Keepers turn up uninvited at rallies where Ruslan Toktarov speaks, and he has never disowned them, which Gulchekhra Usmonova and Muslim leaders point out. The opposition press says Tulpar's supporters' club pays for their buses; this has not been proven. In 2025 the Talas city prosecutor asked the court to dissolve the association for inciting religious enmity, and the case has been adjourned three times. In a 2025 survey by the Talas State University Institute of Sociology, 7 percent of Talas men aged 18–30 said they sympathized with the group. Commentators often say the real risk is a less careful archbishop after Mar Ephrem.[2][5]

    Historians at Talas State University have compared them with Orthodox street groups elsewhere in the former Soviet Union.[6]
