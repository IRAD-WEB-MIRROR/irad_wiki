    Kapar family

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The Kapar family were an Iradi gospel group from the Chatkal highlands: the father, Orozbek Kapar uulu (born 1950), his two daughters and his son, Mattay Orozbek uulu (1978–2007), the guitarist. Between 1993 and 2007 they joined the highland three-voice hymn (üch ün), with its yodelled top voice, to American Southern gospel quartet harmony, over guitar and komuz. Their cassette Chatkaldan ("From Chatkal"; Ak Jol Records, 1996) is in every grandmother's kitchen.[1]

    History

    The family were regulars on IradOne's Swift Hooves Hour and toured American churches between 1995 and 2003, and received a special prize at the Altyn-Zhylky Awards in 1999. The Second Iradi Civil War ended the group. Mattay rode with the Popular Front and was killed at the Aulie-Ata junction in July 2007, and when the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line was drawn that year the father was left in Chatkal and the daughters in Talas, on the other side of it. Nobody on IradOne has ever said on air how Mattay died.[1][2]

    In December 2019 the Swift Hooves Hour put the family on the air together for the first time in twelve years, with the daughters in the studio and their father on a telephone line from Chatkal. The broadcast, which did not mention Mattay, went out once, and they have not sung together since. Music writers have pointed out that a Nurzhan rapper, Maksim Baranov of Vtoraya Smena, was killed on the other side in the same fighting at Aulie-Ata within days of Mattay; nobody has made a song of it.[2]
