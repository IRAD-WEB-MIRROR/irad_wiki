    The 2021 Regional Security Alert

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The 2021 Regional Security Alert was the security alert declared separately by the authorities of the Irad Special Economic Zone and the Autonomous Tribal Regions in August 2021, after the Taliban returned to power in Afghanistan and US forces withdrew from that country. Because Talas Air Base had been the largest US installation in the region during Operation Enduring Freedom, the withdrawal changed Irad more than its neighbors expected.[1]

    The alert

    Although Irad has no border with Afghanistan, officials on both sides feared that militants would cross through neighboring states. ISEZ forces and El-Choro riders reinforced checkpoints on the mountain passes along the Kazakh, Uzbek and Kyrgyz borders and set up a joint intelligence channel at Kara-Oi, one of the few security arrangements shared by the two administrations. Security around Talas Air Base was also reinforced.[1][2]

    The Kabul airlift

    During the evacuation of Kabul, Talas Air Base served as a waypoint for evacuee flights. About 6,500 Afghans passed through between 17 August and 6 September 2021, housed in hangars and a tent camp on the base before onward flights to Europe and the United States. The few dozen Iradis still working for contractors in Afghanistan, the sapar jigitteri ("traveling lads"), came out on the same flights.[3]

    About 340 evacuees stayed, mostly families with ties to Iradi contractors or with nowhere else to go. Executive directive 2021/14 gave them residence permits as "guests of the Republic" under a 2010 regulation. Most live in Talas and Semiz and speak Dari, and their children attend Talas schools, but they still have no travel documents. Ruslan Toktarov's description of Fergana Rim Muslims as "guests" in December 2024 made the word painful for them.[3]

    Effects on the base

    The base's role had already shifted toward regional logistics and training when US combat operations in Afghanistan ended in 2014. After 2021 the remaining Afghan supply work ended and "over-the-horizon" counter-terrorism operations were added. Estimates of US military personnel in Irad fell from about 5,500 in 2020 to about 3,500 in 2024 (Irad Times), base contract spending fell from about $700 million a year in 2019 to about $450 million, and about 1,200 Iradi base jobs were lost in 2021–2023.[2][4]

    "The Kabul fear"

    The withdrawal gave rise to what Iradis call the Kabul korkunuchu ("the Kabul fear"): the fear that Washington would leave Talas as it had left Kabul, and as it had left Irad in 2006. In an address on 31 August 2021 Governor Erlan Toktarov said: "I saw one empire leave Afghanistan. Irad is not Afghanistan, and Talas is not Bagram." The Democratic Party of Irad began to argue that "the base needs us more now", while the opposition calls Talas "a base without a war". The fear shaped Aidar Maksat's pledge of August 2024 to honor the basing arrangements and sharpened the shock of the 2025 Trump Remarks on Irad.[4]

    Other effects

    The Taliban's 2022 ban on opium poppy cut the supply of Afghan heroin, doubled street prices in southern Irad and sped the switch to synthetic opioids (see Organized crime in Irad). The plan for a US-backed oil pipeline south through Afghanistan was abandoned, after which Chevron scaled back its operations in Irad. The union of Soviet–Afghan War veterans held its largest annual gathering in 2022 (see Soviet–Afghan War veterans in Irad).[2]
