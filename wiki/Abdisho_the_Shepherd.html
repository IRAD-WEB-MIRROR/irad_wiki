    Abdisho the Shepherd

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Mar Abdisho the Shepherd (Iradi: Koychu Abdisho; about 1640–1712) is a saint of the Syro-Iradi Catholic Church, venerated as the priest of the pastures. His feast is on 20 May, when the herds go up to the summer pastures.[1]

    Life

    Abdisho was a priest of the Chatkal who, in the years of the Dzungar raids, had no church and followed the herds, celebrating the Qurbana in a yurt and carrying the holy leaven (malka) from camp to camp in a felt-wrapped pot. He herded his own sheep, took no fee and died on the pastures. The clans that keep the malka say that all their leavens come from his (see Kümöch Sacramental Bread).[1]

    Veneration

    He is buried under the stone cairn of Abdisho-Mazar, below a pass at the head of the Chatkal in the Autonomous Tribal Regions, where herders going up to the pastures stop to pour milk and tie a white rag. The Bishop of the Chatkal Highlands, Mar Narsai Abdykerimov, begins his summer ride through the parishes at the cairn on the saint's feast. Abdisho is the patron of shepherds and herders, of the itinerant clergy and the liturgy in the yurt, and of the malka. He is shown as a priest in a felt cloak and a herder's hat, with a shepherd's crook and the leaven pot and sheep at his feet, and is one of the thirteen saints in the mosaics of the Church of St. Janyl. Herders say Koychu Abdisho kayttarsyn ("may Abdisho the Shepherd bring it back") over a strayed animal.[2]
