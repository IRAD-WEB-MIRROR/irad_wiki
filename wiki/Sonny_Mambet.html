    Sonny Mambet

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Sanjar Mambet uulu (born 1971), billed as Sonny Mambet, is an Iradi country-pop singer from Talas who was the star of the territorial years. Nashville-polished and singing in English and Iradi, he was Artist of the Year at the Altyn-Zhylky Awards three times, and his ballad "Ak Köynök" ("The White Dress", 2000) was the slow dance at Iradi weddings for twenty years.[1]

    Career

    Mambet began in 1993 in the cafés by the gate of Talas Air Base and on IradOne's Swift Hooves Hour, and made two cassettes for Ak Jol Records, Gate Café (1995) and Sonny (1997). From 1998 he worked with a steel guitarist hired from Texas and the Talas session guitarist Rysbek Abdrai uulu as his bandleader, and released the CDs Talas, Tennessee (1999), Two Flags (2002) and Home (2006) on his own imprint. He was Artist of the Year in 1998, 2000 and 2003, sang for the territorial governors and played the base every Fourth of July.[1]

    In 2000 he recorded a polished version of Bakyt Sooronbaev's "Kok-Bel Ashuusu" with strings, which outsold Kök Bel's original many times over. Sooronbaev called him "the singer for the base", and Mambet answered that Kök Bel played "for forty people who can't dance". The two camps, the plain and the polished, divided Talas country music for a decade, and Mambet was not invited to Kök Bel's 2014 reunion.[2]

    Decline and revival

    In 2004 Mambet went to Nashville on a development deal with a small label and recorded an album that was never released; nobody in Irad has heard the "Nashville tape". He came home in 2005, and the end of the territory in 2006 and the war of 2007 took away the audience he had been made for. In October 2011 he drove his car into a ditch on the Trans-Talas Highway while drunk; nobody else was hurt, and he lost his licence. He has since appeared in an advertisement for horse feed and as a guest judge on Jyldyz Jolu.[2]

    The collection Sonny: Twenty Years (Köch Audio, 2018) introduced him to younger listeners, and since about 2022 they have taken him up again, half in mockery and half not; the Talas bar band Taka plays his songs. At the 2022 Altyn-Zhylky ceremony he and Sooronbaev sang together, and the hall stood. "Ak Köynök" was succeeded as the wedding slow dance by Altynai Torobek kyzy's "Ekööbüz" (2021).[2]
