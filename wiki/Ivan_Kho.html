    Ivan Kho

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Ivan Kho (born 1947) is a retired Iradi tenor, a Koryo-saram from Kobozev, now Nurzhan, who was the leading singer of Soviet Irad and the first Iradi Korean to become famous. From 1974 to 1987 he was the voice of VIA Kerbez, the Talas Philharmonic's pop ensemble, and he was the first to sing Vladimir Danilov's "Ogni Kobozeva" ("Lights of Kobozev"), still the song of Nurzhan.[1][2]

    Career

    Kho first sang "Ogni Kobozeva" in 1972, as a student at the conservatory, and joined the newly formed vocal-instrumental ensemble VIA Kerbez ("Graceful") as its tenor in 1974. With its electric guitars, Yunost organ and amplified komuz, the VIA was the republic's pop music for the next fifteen years, and Kho sang in Russian and Iradi on its Melodiya LPs Ogni Kobozeva (1975) and Kerbez (1979). In 1981, at a concert in Tashkent, he sang the Korean folk song "Arirang" without having it cleared, and was kept off all-Union television for two years, which Nurzhan's Koreans have not forgotten.[1]

    After the soprano Gulzada Joldosh kyzy married the ensemble's bandleader, Yusup Abdykadyrov, in 1986, the new songs went to her. Kho left VIA Kerbez in 1987 and sang alone; he and Abdykadyrov did not speak again, and he did not attend Abdykadyrov's funeral in 2019. His solo LP Poyot Ivan Kho ("Ivan Kho Sings", Melodiya, 1988) has his Iradi-language songs on one side and Russian songs on the other. He sang nothing in public during 1992, the year of the siege, gave a farewell concert at the Talas Philharmonic in 1993 and retired.[1][2]

    Later life

    Kho lives in Nurzhan. In 2007, three months after the ceasefire, the Altyn-Zhylky Awards were held in a studio with a single prize, a lifetime award to him. In 2016 he and Gulzada Joldosh kyzy shared a stage for the first time since 1987, at the memorial concert for Danilov, where they sang "Ogni Kobozeva" together; Nurzhan people still take sides in the old quarrel. Radio IradOdin plays his records daily. The Melodiya masters are in Moscow, and the forty-song collection Kho issued by Köch Audio in 2019 was made from IradOne's radio tapes.[2]
