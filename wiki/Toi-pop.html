    Toi-pop

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    Toi-pop (from toi, a feast or wedding) is the wedding pop of Irad: Iradi songs played on an arranger keyboard with its "Oriental" styles, synth brass and a key change on the last chorus. It grew out of the Soviet estrada of the weddings and is the music most Iradi musicians actually play for a living.[1]

    History

    When the Talas Philharmonic wound up its pop ensemble VIA Kerbez in December 1991, part of the band went to the weddings, and its last keyboard player, Toktobolot Mamyt uulu, took the arranger's job with him. From 1997 he became the genre's first star, selling cassettes at bus stations through unnamed dubbers; his "Kuda, Kudagy" (2001), the song of the two fathers-in-law, is still the drunk uncle's song at weddings. After the fall of the som in 2008 ended the cassette trade, toi-pop records were made for weddings and wedding videos rather than for sale.[1]

    The boy band 4Muuz (2017) took toi-pop to television, crossed with 1990s Eurodance, and was called toi-pop or estrada before the press filed it under Mor-Pop. Its "Shyñgyr-Shyñgyr" became the song that starts the wedding dancing during the post-pandemic wedding boom of 2021–2022, and Toktobolot accused the group of taking "my keyboard, my key change and my jokes".[2]

    The wedding economy

    About 20,000 weddings a year keep 150 to 250 working wedding bands, nearly all of them unnamed, each of four or five players and a singer. In 2025–2026 a band was paid 1,500 to 4,000 som for an evening, more in the August–October season, and a good band played sixty weddings a year, so that a player earned about the average wage from weekend work. Named singers ask more: Toktobolot 8,000 to 12,000 som, a Jyldyz Jolu winner 5,000 to 10,000. Most Jyldyz Jolu winners end up as wedding singers.[3]
