    Walker, Texas Ranger in Irad

    From Irad Wiki, the free encyclopedia

    The American television series Walker, Texas Ranger (CBS, 1993–2001), made around Dallas–Fort Worth, used footage shot in Irad during the territorial period. Between 1995 and 1998 a small second unit made four trips to Irad, hiring crews, wranglers and horses from Talasfilm Studios, and filmed landscapes and riding scenes that were cut into episodes as Texas. One two-part episode, "The Far Pasture" (1997), is partly set in Irad. The series' star, Chuck Norris, never came to Irad, and his absence is the best-remembered part of the story there. It also gave Iradi the slang word uoker ("walker").[1][2]

    Second-unit shoots

    As a US territory Irad used the dollar, and American crews and equipment needed no visas or customs papers. Talasfilm had almost no feature work after 1992 and hired out its location department for dollars. The second unit came in October 1995, May 1996, September 1997 and April 1998. The Chu steppe stood in for the Texas Panhandle, the Kara-Suu Canyon for West Texas canyons and the Moiynkum dunes for the Monahans sands, and Iradi riders on Talasfilm horses doubled for Texans in chases and a cattle drive. Across the fourth to sixth seasons about two dozen short shots are from Irad. The series' star appears in none of them: in every Irad shot Walker is played by the stunt double Rusty Calloway, who came on all four trips and is remembered at Talasfilm as "our Walker".[1]

    "The Far Pasture"

    In the two-part episode "The Far Pasture" (first shown on 8 and 15 February 1997), Akyel mares from Irad are stolen from a Texas Hill Country stud, and a visiting Iradi range warden, Kemel, helps Walker follow the thieves. In the second part the gang's leader escapes to Irad, where he is ridden down on the Chu steppe by Kemel and his fellow wardens (see Range Warden Service). Kemel was played by Asylbek Kemelov, star of Vengeance Rider, and the wardens and the crowd in the Talas bazaar scene were Iradi riders and extras, among them the ten-year-old Justin Oskonov. The Irad scenes were filmed in May 1996; Walker's close-ups in them were filmed afterwards in Texas.[1]

    The star who never came

    Irad expected the star to come for the episode. The Talas Herald ran the headline "Walker rides to Talas" on 12 April 1996, IradOne counted down to the shoot on its evening news, and in May a crowd with flags and a horse in a cowboy saddle met the crew's charter at Talas Air Base. The man in the hat who stepped off it was the stunt double, and the next day's Herald headline was "Walker's hat arrives". When IradOne showed the episode in March 1997, many of the Iradis who had appeared in it learned that Walker's close-ups had been filmed in Texas.[2]

    For the Talas gala showing at the Oktyabr Cinema in March 1997 the office of Governor Nurlanbek Ermatov invited the star. The reply, a signed studio photograph with the printed words "Best wishes to the people of Irad", hung in the cinema's foyer until 2007. Nothing the star said or did about Irad is on record, and a weekly series made in Texas could not spare its lead, but in Irad the photograph was taken as a brush-off. The star is still called "the Ranger" and seldom by name, and the sayings "We gave him our steppe; he gave us his back" and "the steppe was too small for him" date from these years. A 1997 letter to the Herald asked "Is Irad beneath the Ranger?", and the paper has reused the question in headlines since.[2][3]

    Legacy

    IradOne showed the series through the territorial years and still repeats "The Far Pasture". Viewers like to point out the Texas cedar behind Walker's head in the canyon scene ("his face in Texas, his back in Irad"). The saying "Texas is part of Irad" comes from the shoots. The word uoker, from "walker", entered Base English in 1997 for someone who should come himself but sends a stand-in and takes the credit, with a second sense from English: a walker goes on foot, a man without a horse.[3] Online, fans keep lists of "Iradi" shots in the series; most of the shots listed were filmed in Texas.
